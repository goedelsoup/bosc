<p class="walk-onramp-lead">
  On <strong>19 July 2023</strong> three township trustees in Wood County, Ohio went into executive
  session to hear "a presentation by <strong>Project Accordion</strong> personnel." Two months later
  the same board voted to support a <strong>75 per cent, fifteen-year</strong> tax abatement for
  "<strong>Liames LLC</strong> for a data center," which the county granted on 28 September 2023.
  The operator's name — <strong>Meta</strong> — became public on <strong>9 April 2025</strong>,
  nineteen months later. This story reads that gap out of the minutes of the smallest public bodies
  in Ohio, and teaches you to read them yourself.
</p>

**"Project Accordion" is not our name for this story.** It is the developer's own NDA-era codename,
and it is in a public record: the township's own minutes. Like Fort Wayne's Project Zodiac and
unlike Findlay's Flagpole, there is a cloak here, and the record is where it slips.

What the instruments show, in the order a reader would meet them:

1. **The land was zoned in the sellers' names.** Search this township's minutes for "Liames" and you
   find nothing before September 2023 — yet by then the abatement application already covered "the
   *rezoned* properties." The rezonings are docketed as **Tim Getz, ~80 acres** and the **Busch
   family, ~44 acres**. Learn why a name-keyed search misses the first eighteen months of a project
   like this one.
2. **The abatement was priced on half the jobs.** Wood County Resolution **23-01249** was moved on
   a stated **50 full-time jobs** and a **$4,000,000** payroll. Meta's own letter now says "at least
   100." Learn to tell what a resolution *authorizes* from what an agreement *covenants* — and why
   the agreement itself is still unread.
3. **The vote everyone reported backwards.** On 7 July 2026 the trustees rezoned thirteen parcels.
   The reported tally was 2–1 in favour. The minutes record **1–2**, on a motion to adopt the zoning
   commission's **denial**, with the trustee named as the dissenter voting *yes*. Learn to read a
   roll call against the question it was actually put on.
4. **Three hundred and fifty megawatts, no hearings.** The campus is powered behind the meter by a
   gas plant certified under an accelerated letter-of-notification track that holds no adjudicatory
   hearing at all — and whose certificate then *bars* it from the transmission grid. Learn the
   difference between a docket that is empty and one that is blocked.
5. **A city that sells water, studying how to buy it.** Bowling Green drinks the Maumee and
   discharges to the Portage, wholesales water to its neighbours, and after a flash drought began
   studying an emergency interconnection with Toledo — while committing new industrial supply.
   Learn why two published water figures for the same campus differ by **twelve times**, and why
   neither the withdrawal register nor the discharge permit can settle it.

<article class="prose walk-orientation">
  <h2 id="methodology">How the record is read</h2>
  <p>
    Middleton Township publishes every set of minutes it takes; all <strong>115</strong> files for
    2023–2026 were swept, and the fifteen that name Liames or Project Accordion are why several
    findings here are <em>negatives</em> — a negative is only worth stating when the search behind
    it is. The township's minutes carry text layers and are quoted from them. The county
    commissioners' journal does not: it is a copier scan, read by OCR at 300 DPI, and every figure
    taken from it is graded accordingly and quoted rather than paraphrased.
  </p>
</article>
