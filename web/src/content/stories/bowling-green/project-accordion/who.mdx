Start with the land, because the land is public. In Wood County, Ohio, twelve contiguous parcels in
Middleton Township — **775.020 deeded acres**, whose union is a **single polygon** measuring about
1,821 by 2,489 metres — all carry the same owner of record: **LIAMES, LLC**. `[verified]`

**Eight of the twelve mail their tax bills to `1 META WAY, MENLO PARK, CA 94025`.** `[verified]`
That is Meta's own headquarters, sitting in a county tax field, independent of any announcement —
and four of the twelve go somewhere else entirely, to `52 E GAY ST, COLUMBUS`. A billing address is
not ownership of the LLC, and this chapter is partly about learning to feel the difference.

## What the layer proves, and the three things it cannot

The register is compiled from the Wood County auditor's CAMA parcel layer, and reading it honestly
means reading its limits first.

1. **It keeps only the LAST conveyance per parcel.** Every grantor is therefore invisible. The two
   2025-04-09 quitclaim consolidations that carry 519 of the 775 acres folded in eleven predecessor
   tracts and **erased their purchase prices**. The $6,921,460 of consideration that survives is a
   floor on a fraction — never what the assembly cost.
2. **It has no `editingInfo`, so you date it from the data.** The maximum sale date across all
   73,839 rows is **2025-07-25**, with zero rows after it. Every negative you draw from this layer
   is a statement about July 2025.
3. **It serves one row per polygon PART.** Deduping on the parcel id drops land; summing the
   acreage double-counts. Union the geometry, take the acreage once.

> **The skill:** telling a *compiled register* from an *instrument read*. This is a chain assembled
> from a county's tax layer, not a stack of deed images — which is why it lives in a different
> genre in this library, and why "who sold it" is still an open question at the end of the chapter.

## The resolution that says "data center" in 2023

Then read the record the land was granted an abatement under. On **2023-09-28**, Wood County
Commissioners adopted **Resolution 23-01249** — "AUTHORIZE COMMUNITY REINVESTMENT AREA AGREEMENT
BETWEEN LIAMES LLC AND WOOD COUNTY, OHIO" — a **75 per cent, fifteen-year** exemption, moved on a
stated **50 full-time jobs** and a **$4,000,000** annual payroll. `[verified]`

The county's economic-development director told the board that "Liames LLC plans to construct **one
or more data center buildings** on property located at State Route 582 and Mercer Road." Nineteen
months before Meta's name became public, the *use* was on the record and the *operator* was not.
The chapter ends where the record ends: the Ohio Secretary of State's business search refuses
automated retrieval, so Liames LLC's organizers and statutory agent are **unread**, and the Meta
attribution rests on Meta's own announcement — not on a corporate filing. `[open]`
