<p class="walk-onramp-lead">
  Findlay, Ohio does everything to one small river. It <strong>impounds</strong> the Blanchard into
  two upground reservoirs, <strong>augments</strong> its low flow from them, <strong>discharges</strong>
  a 15-million-gallon-a-day sewage plant into it at a point where the state's own arithmetic says
  there is no dilution left, and has spent roughly <strong>$160 million</strong> trying to stop it
  from flooding downtown. Four miles north, behind a township line that had no zoning at all until
  2026, sits a <strong>150-megawatt</strong> contracted compute load. This story reads all of it out
  of the public record, and teaches you to read it yourself.
</p>

<div class="walk-promise">
  <p class="walk-promise-title">Every figure here is checkable.</p>
  <p>
    Each number is read from a cited record and tagged for confidence —
    <strong>[verified]</strong> when a document states it, <strong>[inference]</strong> when we
    derive it (with the assumption shown), <strong>[open]</strong> when the record doesn't say, and

  </p>
</div>

**"Flagpole" is our name for this story, not anyone else's.** Unlike Fort Wayne's Project Zodiac,
there is no developer codename here to uncover: the host and the customer are both named in SEC
filings, and the county's biggest employer is a Fortune 500 company headquartered downtown. What is
hidden in Findlay is not who — it is *how much*, *under whose contract*, and *against what rule*.

Six chapters, six kinds of record, six skills:

1. **Who is running 150 megawatts up there?** — read a withdrawn registration statement for what a
   disclosure proves, and what it doesn't.
2. **On whose contract does it sit?** — read a utility tariff off the filed sheets, and tell a
   jurisdictional negative from a search that was blocked.
3. **What did the township actually vote for?** — read a zoning book for what it does *not* say.
4. **Where does the river go?** — find the denominator, and learn why the obvious gage was the
   wrong one.
5. **Did the phosphorus number hold?** — rebuild a regulated quantity from its own published
   equation, and know when your reconstruction can't call the year.
6. **What has the river already cost?** — read a public-works program as an instrument chain, and
   follow it downstream.
