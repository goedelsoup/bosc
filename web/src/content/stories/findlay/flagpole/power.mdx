Chapter 1 left a disclosed 150 megawatts and one more sentence from the same filing:

> "In 2024, we expanded the Findlay Megawatt Hub to make available an additional **300 MW** of
> capacity with a standalone interconnection site." `[verified]`

Four hundred and fifty megawatts, in a county of 75,000 people, from a company that withdrew the
filing four months later. This chapter is about what it takes to find out whether that is real — and
what it means when the one place the answer would be filed won't let you in.

## Start with the rule, not the reporting

The relevant instrument is **AEP Ohio's Schedule DCT**, the data-center tariff, and there is a great
deal written about it that is close to right. The version that governs lives at **P.U.C.O. No. 22,
Original Sheet Nos. 223-1 through 223-7**, inside the utility's own filed tariff book. Read there,
four things bear directly on this site.

**It names cryptocurrency mining outright.** The schedule's "Mobile Data Center" definition reaches
electronic information services "(including mining of cryptocurrency)" with portable or distributable
load above **25,000 kW**. MARA's contracted 150,000 kW is six times that threshold. `[verified]`

**A Mobile Data Center customer must swear an oath.** Before receiving service it must provide "a
sworn statement, under penalty of perjury, that neither the customer nor any of its affiliates is
owned or controlled by … any foreign adversary as defined in 15 C.F.R. § 7.4." `[verified]` It is the
only attestation anywhere in the schedule.

**The +300 MW cannot ride a grandfather.** The schedule defines Existing Load that signs a new
electric service agreement to expand by **more than 25,000 kW** as *New Load*. Three hundred megawatts
is twelve times that trigger. `[inference]` — the conclusion follows from the sheets; the premise, that
an executed pre-tariff agreement exists at all, is `[open]`.

**And the famous number is not what it is usually quoted as.** "Data centers must pay for 85% of
contracted capacity for 12 years" is a shorthand that is not precise enough to quote. The 85% is a
*floor*, applied during a ramp (Year 1 50%, Year 2 65%, Year 3 80%, Year 4 90% of Contract Capacity)
and afterwards to a rolling 11-month peak or a Minimum Demand table. Existing Load bills at **60%**,
not 85%. Term is the ramp (up to four years) plus eight. `[verified]`

> **The skill:** read the tariff sheets, not the press summary. Every one of the four points above
> is available in secondary coverage in a form that is subtly wrong — the threshold, the oath, the
> percentage, the term. The sheets are public, they are seven pages, and they settle it.

## Who is the customer, though?

Here the tariff runs out. One Power owns the site and its own transmission-voltage substation; MARA
leases capacity behind it. Whether Schedule DCT reaches the **host**, the **tenant**, or neither turns
on an electric service agreement that nobody has produced. `[open]` That single unproduced document
would settle three questions at once — who the DCT customer is, whether the site is Existing or New
Load, and whether the +300 MW is contracted at all.

## Two things that look like the answer and aren't

**Rocky Ford Station is not this load's interconnection.** In 2024 a fresh **138 kV** station went up
in Hancock County — two OPSB cases, both filed 2024-09-10: **24-0707-EL-BLN** (AEP Ohio Transco, the
6.25-acre station, approximately **$8,700,000**) and **24-0706-EL-BNR** (Ohio Power Company, the cut-in
and tie line, approximately **$2,214,000**), all reimbursed by the interconnecting customer.
`[verified]` It is tempting, and wrong, to read a new substation as the hub's service. Both filings
state the need themselves: it serves **Border Basin I, LLC**, an independent power producer building
a solar plant near **Arcadia** — PJM queue position **AE1-146**, in **Cass Township**, not the Allen
Township where the Megawatt Hub sits. `[verified]` That is a *generation* interconnection. A generator
queue is not a load queue, and a new station four townships over is not evidence about a compute
campus.

**"No OPSB case for the Central Findlay work" is a jurisdictional fact, not a search failure.** AEP's
own scope for that rebuild is **69 kV** — four miles rebuilt, two miles of new overhead, one mile of
new underground. R.C. 4906.01(B)(2) starts OPSB jurisdiction at transmission lines "of one hundred
kilovolts or more." A 69 kV project generates no case because it can generate none. `[verified]`

> **The skill:** before recording an absence, ask whether the record you searched could ever have
> contained the thing. A negative that follows from a statute is worth `[verified]`. A negative that
> follows from a search is worth exactly as much as the search was.

## The firewall

Which brings us to the one route that would actually decide it.

A Findlay large-load instrument — an ESA, a letter of agreement, an intervention in the tariff
docket — would be filed at the **Public Utilities Commission of Ohio**. PUCO's Docketing Information
System, `dis.puc.state.oh.us`, **rejects automated retrieval outright**: every request, with and
without a browser user-agent, referer, or session cookie, returns an application-firewall "Request
Rejected" page. PUCO's own news page for the July 2025 order 404s; only a GovDelivery mirror survives.

    across seven slides; only zone totals. AEP's 2026-02-13 report to the PUCO (5,642 MW signed under

    County, One Power, nor MARA. Those three are genuine <code>[verified — negative]</code> results.
    The fourth — the docket — <strong>was never searched</strong>. Its silence proves nothing, and is
    written down as proving nothing.

## What you've established

The rule that would govern a 150 MW crypto load in AEP Ohio's territory exists, names cryptocurrency
by name, demands a sworn oath, and would treat a 300 MW expansion as new load no matter what came
before. Whether it binds *this* site is unresolved, because the contract is private and the docket is
closed to machines.

So the +300 MW stands where it started: **one sentence, in a withdrawn registration statement, whose
filing never mentions AEP or PJM anywhere.** Read it as a developer's availability claim to a
prospective investor. Never as documented grid capacity. `[open]`
