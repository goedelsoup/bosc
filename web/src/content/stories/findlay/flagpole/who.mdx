Most of the campuses in this network were assembled quietly, through Delaware shells, and the first
job is figuring out who is behind them. Findlay is the opposite case. The **customer** described the
facility in a press release on **2024-11-11**; the **host** described the same facility in a
registration statement filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission about ten weeks later, on
**2025-01-23**. The numbers agree. That makes this the network's cleanest **disclosed** load — and it
changes the skill you need. When the developer tells you the number, the question stops being *who*
and becomes *what exactly did they commit to, in what document, and what happened to that document
afterward*.

## The facility, from both sides

**One Power Co** — privately held, CEO Jereme Kent — owns a campus in **Allen Township, Hancock
County**, at I-75 and Township Road 215, about four miles north of downtown Findlay. Its Form S-1
calls the facility **MWHub 01, the "Findlay Megawatt Hub,"** and describes it in the register's own
vocabulary: `[verified]`

> Current Capacity 30 MW · Planned Maximum 150 MW · Status: **Operating**

First energized in 2023 with what the filing calls "the first fully digital substation in the United
States." The anchor customer is named in the same document: **MARA Holdings, Inc.** (NASDAQ: MARA),
a bitcoin miner, on a **150 MW, 15-year, take-or-pay** lease — payment "due regardless of whether or
not the customer elects to purchase power." `[verified]`

The customer had already said it, independently and ten weeks earlier. MARA's release of
**2024-11-11** describes "a 150-megawatt operation in Findlay, Ohio, which already has 30 megawatts
of capacity," one of three Ohio sites totalling ~372 MW, with full energization "intended by
end-2025." `[verified]`

> **The skill:** two-sided corroboration. A single company's description of its own project is a
> claim. The *same* figure, from the counterparty, in a separate document issued for a different
> purpose and a different audience, is a fact. Note the two documents are not the same *kind* of
> record: MARA's is an investor-relations press release, One Power's a registration statement signed
> under securities-law liability. That is why the 150 MW here is `[verified]` while every
> acreage-derived load in this network — Urbana's, Sidney's, Van Wert's — is `[inference]`.

Whether the full 150 MW is energized *today* is `[open]`. "Intended by end-2025" is a plan, and no
reviewed source confirms the build-out completed. That is the standing check on this facility.

## The guard that has to come first

<div class="callout" id="mara-guard">
  <p class="callout-title">MARA Holdings ≠ Marathon Petroleum</p>
  <p>
    <strong>MARA Holdings, Inc.</strong> (NASDAQ: MARA), formerly Marathon Digital Holdings, is a
    bitcoin-mining company and the 150 MW customer here. <strong>Marathon Petroleum Corp</strong>
    (NYSE: MPC) is a petroleum refiner <em>headquartered in Findlay</em> and a Hancock County
    NPDES and air permittee. They are unrelated. Both attach to the string "Marathon … Findlay,
    Ohio," and a graph that merges them would attribute a refinery's permits to a data center in
    the same county. The entity graph carries an explicit disambiguation note on both records.
  </p>
</div>

This is not a hypothetical. It is exactly the trap an automated entity resolver falls into, and it
is the reason this chapter opens with a name rather than a number.

## The filing that stopped

The S-1 is the primary disclosure, and its own history is part of the record:

- Form **S-1 filed 2025-01-23** (EDGAR CIK 2039139; confidential draft submission 2024-11-12).
- **Withdrawn** — Form RW — **2025-05-09**. The IPO never went effective.
- Form **D** private placement, **2025-07-23**. `[reference]`
- A September 2025 reorganization, layoffs, and a lender workout. `[reference]`
- **2026-02-16: OnSite Partners acquires One Power Company.** OnSite is owned by funds advised by
  **Basalt Infrastructure Partners**. `[verified]`

Read that sequence as a whole and it says something the individual items don't: a developer that
tried the public markets, withdrew, restructured under lender pressure, and was bought by an
infrastructure fund — while holding a fifteen-year take-or-pay contract that pays whether or not the
tenant draws power. The contract is the asset. That is a `[reference]`-grade reading of a corporate
chain, not a finding about anyone's solvency, and it is written here as such.

<div class="callout callout--open">
  <p class="callout-title">A withdrawn filing is still evidence — of a specific kind</p>
  <p>
    An S-1 that never went effective was still signed, and still carries liability for what it
    states about the company's existing operations. Its account of a running facility is strong.
    Its account of what the company <em>plans</em> is a marketing document that no regulator ever
    tested — which is precisely the distinction Chapter 2 turns on.
  </p>
</div>

## The land, and the sixty-one acres that don't reconcile

The holding of record is committed:
**eight Allen Township parcels, 108.65 acres by county CAMA / 105.873 acres planar**, standing in
**three One Energy vehicles** — `ONE ENERGY ENTERPRISES LLC` (the 4.07-acre office at 12411 TR 215),
`OEE XX LLC` (69.82 acres at CR 216), and `OEE XXX LAND LLC` (six parcels on TR 99). It is two blocks
about 200 metres apart; the north block is **73.89 acres**, matching the reported 74-acre wind campus,
and **seven of Hancock County's ten wind turbines** stand inside it. `[verified]`

The S-1 calls the campus **"~170 acres."** The record shows 108.65. **The 61-acre gap is not closed
here, and no parcel was invented to close it.** It is consistent with a reported 2025 40-acre
purchase plus land held by lease, option, or a vehicle whose tax-mailing label reads neither `OEE`
nor `ONE ENERGY`. Likewise **12385 TR 215**, the hub's own address in its SEC filing, appears in no
parcel in the source at all — the nearest situs is the office at 12411. `[open]`

## Why the deed chain is missing, precisely

Press reporting puts **110 acres closed 2026-03-05 for $5.9 million** in the North Findlay Industrial
Park, plus 40 acres in 2025. `[reference]` The grantee vehicle for both is `[open]` — and the reason
matters more than the gap:

- Hancock County publishes **no parcel ArcGIS REST service**. The only machine-readable source is the
  OGRIP Ohio statewide public view, whose Hancock slice is a **2023-05-08 export** — it predates both
  closings.
- The Auditor's live CAMA is **Beacon/Schneider only** and returns **Cloudflare HTTP 403** to every
  non-browser request.
- The Recorder's index is **Kofile CountyFusion**, whose guest login 404s outside a browser session.

Both were probed on 2026-08-02.

> **The skill:** distinguishing *unsearched* from *empty*. "No grantee found" reads like a negative
> finding and is nothing of the kind here — the deed exists, it is public, and two county systems
> simply refuse automated retrieval. The honest record says so, names the instrument to pull, and
> leaves the tag `[open]`. A negative check is only worth `[verified]` when the search actually ran.

## What you've established

There is an operating **30 MW** compute load with **150 MW contracted** on a fifteen-year take-or-pay,
in a township, north of a city, disclosed by both parties to the SEC. The land of record is 108.65
acres in three affiliated LLCs; the developer's own acreage claim exceeds it by 61 acres that the
public record does not yet explain. The developer withdrew its IPO and was acquired.

None of that came from a press release. It came from a registration statement, a counterparty's
release, and a county parcel export — and from being explicit about the two places the chain breaks.
