Findlay's data-center question has three governments in it, and the useful finding is a negative:
**as of this reading, none of them has an adopted rule that reaches the 150 MW already contracted.**

The county's instrument bars large wind and solar and is silent on load. The City of Findlay's
moratorium reaches data centers and stops at the corporation line. **Allen Township — where the hub
and the take-or-pay actually sit — had no zoning at all until 2026-05-11**, and the resolution that
took effect that day does not contain the phrase "data center" once in seventy-seven pages.

Eleven weeks later, its zoning commission moved language that would permit data centers in two
industrial districts — capped at **10 MW**.

## Zoned by wind, amended for compute

The fight that made Allen Township zoned had nothing to do with data centers. The appellate court's
own background says so: in late 2023 One Energy "was making plans to construct additional wind
turbines in Allen Township to, inter alia, provide power to the Whirlpool manufacturing facility."
Residents asked about zoning. The trustees opposed renaming Township Road 215 to "Electric Avenue"
without meeting in public, stayed after an adjourned meeting to discuss who should sit on a zoning
commission, and were sued twice for it.

They lost, and so did One Energy. **2026-Ohio-405** affirms both open-meetings violations and the
prospective injunction — *and* affirms the trial court's refusal to unwind the zoning commission,
because the December 2023 resolutions had been rescinded, which "breaks any causal link," and the
selection criteria "were not unique." The opinion's own line: *"This Court declines to enjoin the use
of common sense."* `[verified]`

That opinion incidentally corroborates Chapter 2: the Wind-for-Industry turbines serve an industrial
host's meter. Nothing places generation at the Megawatt Hub, and now a court's findings say so too.

The resolution the trustees adopted on **2024-09-09** then sat for twenty months. A first referendum
passed in May 2025 and was undone over abbreviated ballot language — the township's own minutes record
that its "zoning status was revoked by Judge Jonathon Starn." The second referendum, **2026-05-05**,
carried:

|---|---|---|
| **For the zoning resolution** | **503** | 69.48% |
| Against | 221 | 30.52% |

Certified **2026-05-11** — the same date Article XXIV of the zoning book gives as its own effective

> **The skill:** go to the certified canvass. The widely reported unofficial figure was **502**–221.
> One vote changes nothing about the outcome and everything about whether your record can be trusted
> on the details. And read the canvass carefully: this Board of Elections prints each contest title
> **below** its own results, so a naive top-to-bottom read misattributes every block.

## What the electorate actually approved

An **enumerated-use** scheme — *"no building or land shall be used and no building shall be erected
except for one or more of the following specified uses"* — across nine districts, with no mention of
data centers anywhere in it. Section 1518's prohibited-in-all-districts list is adult entertainment,
commercial marijuana, and private landfills. Non-accessory solar and non-accessory energy storage are
conditional uses in I-2 only. `[verified]`

Four weeks before the vote, on **2026-04-07**, the commission chairman asked the township's own
counsel whether the pending resolution would permit a data center. The minutes record the answer in
one sentence: **"Cindy stated that a data center would not be permitted."** `[verified]`

Three weeks after it took effect, the same counsel recommended drafting data-center and BESS language.

> **The skill:** in an enumerated-use code, absence *is* the rule. What a zoning book does not list,
> it does not permit. Counting to zero across seventy-seven pages is a finding, and it is the kind
> a full-text search can make in a minute and a summary will never tell you.

## A 10 MW cap over a 150 MW site

On **2026-07-28** the zoning commission adopted Resolution **24-04-124M**, 5-0, proposing a new
**Section 1521, DATA CENTERS**. The provisions that matter: `[verified]`

- **Conditional use in I-1 and I-2 only**, approved by the Board of Zoning Appeals.
- **Maximum Electrical Capacity: 10 MW Total Facility Load at any single site**, measured against
  interconnection agreements, engineering plans, or nameplate capacity.
- **Anti-aggregation:** phases count as one site if on contiguous parcels *or* "Sharing electrical
  infrastructure, substations, or utility interconnections," and "The combined load of all phases
  shall not exceed the 10 MW limit."
- **Water:** no groundwater wells at all; municipal or approved public-utility supply only, with
  written capacity verification; a **100,000 GPD** ceiling.
- **Cooling:** "All data centers shall be air cooled (dry cooling)" — evaporative, once-through *and*
  closed-loop liquid systems all expressly prohibited.
- One mile from another data center, 1,250 feet from residential use, 60% lot coverage, 40 dBA at the
  property line, generators tested weekdays 8–6 only.

The disclosed facility at this site is 150 MW contracted with 30 MW energized. The proposal is an
order of magnitude below the contract, three times below what is already running, and its aggregation
rule is written against precisely the architecture of a shared megawatt hub.

Three drafting observations are recorded as `[inference]` and are worth a hearing comment rather than
a headline: the permitted "dry cooling" and the prohibited "closed-loop liquid" describe overlapping
equipment; §1521 assigns approval to a Board of Zoning Appeals that does not hold that power until the
*other* hearing the same evening moves it there; and its anti-circumvention subsection assigns duties
to a "Zoning Administrator," an office the adopted book does not have.

## The county restricts supply; the city stops at its line

Hancock County moved in 2022 to create an **SB 52 restricted area** over its unincorporated
territory; the *proposed* map excepts Biglick Township. `[reference]` The scope point is the one that
matters, and it *is* `[verified]`, from the published notice's own title: R.C. 303.57 restricted areas
prohibit "economically significant wind farms, large wind farms, and large solar facilities." **They
do not reach data centers, or load of any kind.** The county instrument that looks like a siting
control restricts *supply* and leaves *demand* untouched — the same asymmetry Chapter 2 found on the
grid side.

Be precise about what is held, because it is less than it looks. This repo has the
**meeting-setting** Resolution **167-22** of 2022-03-15 (roll call 2-1, Bechtol dissenting — that
roll call belongs to *this* resolution, not to any adoption), the published notice, and the
**proposed** map. **The instrument adopted at the 2022-04-19 meeting is not in the corpus at all** —
its resolution number, its roll call, and its final map are each `[open]`, because the county's
online agenda archive begins in 2024. So the coverage above rests on the proposed map and would have
to be re-checked against the adopted one; only the *scope* survives that caveat, because it comes
from the notice rather than the map.

And the city's **Ordinance 2026-42**, the twelve-month data-center moratorium the issue was named for,
**cannot be read**. `findlayohio.gov` returns HTTP 403 to any automated request; American Legal's code
library does the same; `findlay.legistar.com` resolves to an unprovisioned tenant that answers
*"LegistarConnectionString setting is not set up in InSite for client: findlay."* So the ordinance is
held only as two newspapers describe it, and one of those hedges its own roll call mid-sentence
(*"DeArment (or DeLong per some records)"*) — which is why that roll call is not published here.

> **The skill:** an access failure is not a denial, and the response is the ordinary one. A records
> request under R.C. 149.43 is drafted and sitting in the corpus, unsent — sending it is the
> requester's own act, not the archive's.

## What you've established

A 150 MW load arrived first. The rules came after, from three governments with three different
reaches, in three different states of completion: one adopted but aimed at generation, one adopted
but unreadable and bounded by the city limits, and one that is still a proposal — and would cap the
site at one-fifteenth of what is contracted there, if it passes, and if it applies.
