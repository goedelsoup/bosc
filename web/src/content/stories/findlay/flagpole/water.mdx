Every water finding in this network is a fraction. The numerator is what a facility puts in the
river; the denominator is what the river has to dilute it with. Get the denominator wrong and every
number downstream of it is wrong by the same factor — which is exactly what happened here, and how it
was caught.

## The plant

The **City of Findlay Water Pollution Control Center** sits at 1201 South River Road and discharges
through a single final outfall, `2PD00008001`, to the **Blanchard River at River Mile 56.42**.
Constructed 1988, last upgraded 2001. Average design flow **15 MGD**, peak hydraulic capacity **40
MGD**. It serves Findlay, the villages of Arcadia and Van Buren, and parts of Hancock County. The
collection system is 93% separate and 7% combined, with **ten authorized combined sewer overflows** —
eight to the Blanchard and two to **Eagle Creek**, the same Eagle Creek Chapter 6's flood program is
building a dry storage basin on. Ten sanitary sewer overflows are separately monitored. `[verified]`

## There is no dilution

Table 12 of the fact sheet gives the design low flows **at the outfall**: `[verified]`

| Statistic | Value (cfs) |
|---|---|
| 1Q10, annual | 0.17 |
| **7Q10, annual** | **0.21** |
| 30Q10, summer | 0.31 |
| 30Q10, winter | 1.24 |
| 90Q10, annual | 0.32 |
| Harmonic mean, annual | 1.84 |
| **Findlay WPCC average design flow** | **23.208** |

The plant's design flow is roughly **110 times** the river's annual 7Q10. Ohio EPA does the
arithmetic itself, on page 13:

> Acute Dilution Ratio = (1Q10 + WWTP flow rate) / WWTP flow rate = (0.17 cfs + 23.2 cfs) / 23.2 cfs
> = **1.0**

A ratio of 1.0 is the floor of that calculation. It means that at design flow, below River Mile 56.42,
**the Blanchard River is the effluent.** The acute wasteload allocation that follows is expressed as
30 percent mortality in 100 percent effluent. `[verified]`

## The denominator that was wrong

This network used to screen Findlay against **8.67 cfs** — a statistical fit at USGS gage 04189000,
which put the effluent at 2.68× the low flow and produced a dilution ratio of 0.37:1. That number is
retired for this reach, on two grounds neither of which can be argued around: `[verified]`

**The gage sits below the outfall.** USGS 04189000 is at 41.05589 / −83.68799, about 1.1 miles
west-northwest of the outfall at 41.049722 / −83.667778, on a reach the Blanchard runs westward.
Straub's own station description places it "2.0 mi west of Findlay, 3.0 mi downstream from Eagle
Creek." **So the flow that denominator measured already contained the discharge it was being used to
screen.**

**And the gage is regulated.** Straub's REMARKS for the station, verbatim:

> "Water is diverted upstream from station into Findlay Reservoir. Storage in Findlay Reservoir used
> for water supply of city of Findlay, and is available for low-flow augmentation. All water returns
> to stream upstream from station."

That is the city's own two upground reservoirs — among Ohio's largest — holding back the river and
letting it out again. A gage downstream of a municipal augmentation scheme measures a *managed* flow,
not the river's natural low flow. Using it as an assimilative denominator credits the discharger with
water the city itself is releasing.

Where the Blanchard is genuinely **unregulated**, USGS publishes a 7Q10 of **0.03 cfs** (gage
04188337, below Mt. Blanchard) and **0 cfs** for its Eagle Creek tributary (04188496). The 0.21 cfs
Ohio EPA computed at RM 56.42 is consistent with those. The 8.67 never was.

> **The skill:** interrogate the gage before you use its number. Two questions settle most of it — *is
> it upstream or downstream of the thing I am screening*, and *is the flow it measures regulated by
> anybody?* Both answers are printed in the station's own metadata, and both were printed here.

Corrected, the screen reads **0.009:1** — about **41× tighter** than the figure it replaced, effluent
110× the low flow rather than 2.68×.

## What is downstream

Thirty-odd river miles down the Blanchard sits **Ottawa**, another watershed point in this network,
which draws its drinking water from and discharges its own effluent into the same river. The committed
one-river comparison holds both plants against each other on each one's own fact sheet — not on a
shared assumption. Whatever Findlay does at RM 56.42 arrives there.

## What you've established

At design flow the Blanchard below Findlay's outfall *is* treated effluent, by the permitting
agency's own arithmetic, printed in the permit's own fact sheet. And the number this network had been
using to describe that river was 41× too generous — not because anyone falsified it, but because it
came from a gage that sat below the outfall on a reach the city augments.

That is the ordinary way a water finding goes wrong, and the ordinary way it gets fixed: by reading
where the number came from.
