Twelve of Lima's thirteen chapters stand on the record. That is an unusually complete
reconstruction, and it makes the one gap sharper rather than softer.

Everything the community gives can now be screened. The water is quantified, at a fifth of the
city's plant output, and it cuts the zero-refill drought reserve from 960.9 days to 761.8. The
heat is quantified, against a reach that can absorb 0.1 MW. The runoff is quantified, at 115
impervious acres feeding a basin that barely attenuates a flood. The air is bounded by
<Cite record="permits/4132514.epa.yaml">a permit cap</Cite> that one plausible runtime band
breaks. The grid share is quantified, at 5.64 % of
a utility serving 1.5 million customers.

What cannot be quantified is what the community gets, and how much it paid for it. The
fiscal chapter is the only gap on this sheet, and it is the half of the trade the public
actually voted on.

## Why the sheet totals nothing

A total requires both columns in the same units. The environmental column is in cubic feet
per second, degrees, acres, and megawatts. The economic column would be in dollars, and the
dollars are the missing chapter.

A number here would have to be invented. This study does not invent numbers, so the sheet
stops at verdicts.
