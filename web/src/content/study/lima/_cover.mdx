A data-center campus is under construction at 4110 N. Cole St., in American and Sugar Creek
Townships, just outside Lima. The parcel covers 335 acres. The developer of record is Google. Two of the
figures this study turns on are not disclosed anywhere, and are read off the air permit
instead: the campus is screened at 250 to 300 megawatts (MW) of computing load, and its
cooling is screened as evaporative because the permit authorizes 36 cooling towers. Chapter 2
shows the working, and every figure derived from those two carries an inference tag.

Before a project of that size receives a tax abatement or a rezoning, a community would
normally read an impact study. It would say how much water the project takes, what its
discharge does to the river, what it does to the power grid, and what the tax trade costs.
Allen County approved the trade. No such study was produced.

This document is that study, rebuilt from the public record. Fourteen of its fifteen chapters
now stand on committed, cited data. The thirteenth is the fiscal chapter, and it is empty
for one reason: the county's own cost-benefit analysis is a public record, and the county
withheld it.

Read the verdict strip below as a map of the record itself. "On the record" means every
ingredient is cited and checkable. "Gap" means a specific document does not exist in public
hands — and each gap names the office that holds it.
