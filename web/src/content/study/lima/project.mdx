The campus sits at 4110 N. Cole St., on the American Township and Sugar Creek Township line
north of Lima. Ohio EPA files it as Facility 0302022054. The
stormwater permit application declares a parcel of 335 acres, of which 195 acres will be
developed and 115 acres will be permanently impervious. That impervious figure is the one the
stormwater chapter later routes downstream.

Eleven recorded parcels carry the name Bistrozzi LLC, a Delaware limited liability company.
Bistrozzi is the grantee on the corridor deeds and the applicant on the Ohio EPA permits.
Google is the developer of record. Construction is under way.

## What the permits state, and what they withhold

Ohio EPA issued the final air permit-to-install, P0138965, on 2026-05-28. It authorizes 114
backup generators, one per data hall, and 36 cooling towers. Those two counts are the
project's most useful public numbers, because each one scales.

The generator count does not, by itself, give a backup capacity. That needs the rating of a
single engine, and the issued permit does not print it. Ohio EPA granted the applicant a
trade-secret withholding under OAC 3745-49-03 on 2025-10-08, and the rating came out of the
final document. The draft public notice had already published it at about 2,750 electrical
kilowatts per engine. The study therefore reports roughly 313 MW of backup as the final
permit's unit count multiplied by the draft's per-engine rating. It is not a figure the issued
permit states.

The cooling archetype follows the same pattern. Thirty-six cooling towers imply an
evaporative plant, which is the archetype every water chapter is screened against. The
cooling-system flow rates are withheld as confidential business information. So the archetype
is <strong>asserted from the tower count, not disclosed by the operator</strong>, and every
water figure downstream inherits that qualification.
