This chapter holds two different things, and they must not be read as one.

The first is a what-if. It asks what would happen if the campus took its 3.92 MGD of cooling
makeup out of the limestone aquifer instead of the city main. The answer is that the aquifer
could not supply it: the modeled cone reaches the full 47 feet of saturated thickness and
dewaters. The campus does not do this. It buys treated surface water, and no groundwater
withdrawal permit for it is on the record. The cone is a screen, and it is never a headline.

The second is documented, and it already happened.

## The dewatering that ran through the winter

Construction dewatering pumps groundwater out of the ground so crews can work below the water
table. <Cite dataset="ohio-waterwells">Ohio Department of Natural Resources records</Cite> show
44 dewatering wells on this site,
with a combined capacity of 4.9 MGD. They operated from 2025-12-16 to 2026-05-28. One well
was still active at the last check.

The regional water table falls about 11.8 feet per mile toward the northwest. Wells northwest
of the site are therefore down-gradient of it: groundwater moves that way. Ten mapped wells
fall inside the combined cone.

Read those ten by the fraction of water column consumed, not by the drawdown in feet. The
nearest is <Cite dataset="ohio-waterwells" basis="input">a domestic well</Cite> 2,056 feet
away. The model puts 8.9 feet of drawdown on it, which
is less than the 9.9 feet it puts on a well 42 feet farther out. But the near well has only 19
feet of column to give, so that 8.9 feet takes 47 % of it — against 18 % at the farther well,
which holds 55 feet. None of the ten goes dry on these numbers.

## Where the water went is not documented

Pumped groundwater has to go somewhere. Between the Ottawa River gages at Lima and at Kalida,
the field's full capacity would add up to 7.6 cfs. The honest statement of that figure is a
bracket from 0 to 7.6 cfs, because the routing is not on the record. Wells were installed and
sealed in stages, and no discharge record for the field has been produced.

<Callout variant="open">
  One well roughly 3.7 miles down-gradient of the field was reportedly drilled dry and
  replaced. Whether that is connected to the dewatering cannot be answered from this record.
  Answering it needs the contractor's dewatering discharge records and the well owner's own
  log, both tracked on the leads board.
</Callout>
