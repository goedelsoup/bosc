The farms were not bought by the developer. The public **Port Authority of Allen County** took
three-year options on them across 2023–24, at $1,000 apiece, and then assigned those options to
Bistrozzi LLC on 2025-07-03 — using each option's §21 "unrestricted right to assign to a
nominee." The public body held the door; a Delaware LLC walked through it. That is the assembly
model, and it is why a search for the operator's name against the recorder's grantee index
returns nothing until the very end.

## The price the form left blank

The DTE-100 conveyance-fee statements were produced with their value lines blank. The deeds the
county actually recorded carry a second number the parties do not control: a conveyance fee,
stamped by the auditor and set by statute in proportion to the sale price.
<Cite record="recorder/202508130008300.deed.yaml">The Pike Run parcel's deed</Cite> shows a
**$1,041** fee; <Cite record="recorder/202508130008312.deed.yaml">the Neff Farms transfer</Cite>,
**$25,572.90**. A fee that scales with the price is a price in disguise — the figure the DTE-100
left blank is reconstructable from the recorder's own arithmetic at the county's published rate.
`[inference]` — the rate is statutory; the back-calculation is ours.

## The assembly did not close when the public found out

The August 2025 farm deeds were the middle of the chain, not its end. A five-acre Neighbors
parcel passed to Bistrozzi on <Cite record="recorder/202603040002064.deed.yaml">2026-03-04 for a
recited consideration of one dollar</Cite>, and a 7.2-acre Pieper tract was conveyed on
<Cite record="recorder/202604210003890.deed.yaml">2026-04-21</Cite> — not to Bistrozzi LLC but to
**Bistrozzi Addition LLC**, registered with the Ohio Secretary of State two weeks earlier and
organized by the same Vorys attorney behind the original shells. `[verified]`, from the recorded
deeds and the SoS filing.

<Callout variant="takeaway">
  A conveyance chain on the record is what was produced, not what exists. Options, nominee
  agreements, and unrecorded side letters leave no trace in a recorder's index — so the chain
  below is a floor on the assembly, never a ceiling.
</Callout>
