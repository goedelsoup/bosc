The screen above sets the modeled draw against the Ottawa River's cited 7Q10 because that
is the drought floor Ohio writes discharge permits at — but Lima's supply does not come
out of the river in real time. The city draws treated water from five off-stream
reservoirs (~15 billion gallons of storage) filled from the Auglaize and Ottawa at high
flow, so the operating constraint is <strong>reservoir drawdown</strong> across a dry
season, not instantaneous river depletion. That is why the headline reads as a
basin-scale worst-case bound, never a withdrawal claim.

<Callout variant="open">
  What the bound cannot answer is the seasonal question: how fast the campus's draw runs
  the reservoirs down in the months when the rivers cannot refill them — the refill window
  the seasonal-withdrawal annex maps month by month. The metered answer sits in records
  the study still asks for: the campus's water-service contract and its allocated share
  into the City of Lima WWTP, both tracked on the leads board.
</Callout>
