## Six instruments, and not one states a load

That sentence is the whole of this chapter's difficulty, and it is `[verified]` by enumeration
rather than assumed. The state permits that have issued or been filed for this campus are:
construction stormwater coverage **1GC10596\*AG**, isolated-wetland authorization **251911W**,
sanitary-sewer PTI **DSWPTI-260517**, the City's own road wetland permit **252256W**, AES's adjacent
transmission-reroute coverage **1GC11112\*AG**, and — filed 2026-08-03 — air PTI application
**P0141118**. None of them carries a megawatt field at all.

The absence has been tested rather than presumed. Ohio EPA's eDocument portal returns **zero**
air-permit documents for Shelby County under any of the three names this campus is filed as, while
the same query shape returns nineteen Amazon air-permit documents elsewhere in Ohio — so the zero is
a zero and not a broken search. `[verified]`

<Callout variant="takeaway">
  A permit is written to answer its own statute's question. Stormwater coverage asks about
  disturbed acreage; a wetland authorization asks about fill; a sanitary PTI asks about flow. **None
  of them has a reason to ask what the building draws.** A campus can be fully permitted and its
  load never appear, and that is what has happened here.
</Callout>

## Sidney wrote the category, and wrote it wide open

Three months before the campus was zoned, and six before the abatement was signed, the City put
data centers into its zoning code for the first time.
<Cite record="sidney/council/2025-04-28-ord-a-3219-data-center-use-created.resolution.yaml">Ordinance
A-3219</Cite>, adopted **2025-04-28**, Exhibit A, glossary of uses:

> "Warehouse — Data Center. A facility used for the remote storage, processing, or distribution of
> large amounts of data that is **greater than 50,000 square feet**."

And in the same exhibit's use table, one letter in one cell: a **P — permitted by right — in the
Industry / Innovation / Manufacturing column, and nothing in the other eight districts.** Both the
row and the P are printed in red underline, which the exhibit's own legend defines as *NEW*.
`[verified]`

**What follows from a P is the point.** By-right means no conditional-use permit, no variance, no
Board of Zoning Appeals hearing. There is no approval record for this campus's *use* to look for,
because none was ever required — an absence that is structural, and that this study will not report
as a gap in the record.

<Callout variant="takeaway">
  Compare Van Wert, which defined "Data Center" in its code on the same night it annexed and zoned
  its campus, and attached to that zoning exactly one condition: a landscape mound. **Sidney did the
  definitional work fourteen months earlier and attached nothing at all.** Two cities, two
  sequences — and neither instrument imposes a single operating condition on a data center. Write
  both sequences; there is nothing in either record that makes one a response to the other.
</Callout>

The only recorded deliberation on why the category was created is a Planning Commission exchange
from **2025-03-17**, and it is worth reading in full because it is short. A member asked *"why the
proposed code is specifically calling out data centers when the code currently lists warehouse-
small format and large format; noting that a data center should fall under one of those existing
uses."* Staff replied that the city was *"being inclusive to different types of uses that may or may
not come into the city"* and that since *"a data center stores information"* — where warehousing
covers *"the literal storage of physical goods and materials"* — *"staff feels the distinction needs
to be added."* `[verified]`

No project is named. Whether the category was drafted with this campus in view is `[open]`, and the
chronology is left to speak for itself.

A smaller thing the definition does: it sets a **50,000 square foot floor**. A data facility at or
below that size is not a "Warehouse – Data Center" under this code, and no other listed use covers
it. `[open]` — what such a building would be permitted as, and where.

## The number exists. It is filed, and it is withheld

On **2026-04-24** AES Ohio filed a **Construction Service Agreement with Amazon Data Services** at
FERC — docket **ER26-2303-000**, accession `20260424-5179`, described in the docket as "Construction
Service Agreement with Amazon, Sidney OH". The Commission accepted it on **2026-06-23**
(195 FERC ¶61,239). `[verified]`

The megawatt figure is in that agreement. The unredacted twin of the filing, accession
`20260424-5180`, carries **`availCode N`** — non-public. `[verified]`

So the campus load is not "undisclosed". It is **disclosed to a federal regulator and withheld from
the public record, in a document whose accession number is known.** That is a materially sharper
`[open]` than a bare one, and it is the single most answerable ask on this site.

<Callout variant="open">
  Only **Buckeye Power, Inc.** contested the filing. The Ohio Consumers' Counsel did not appear.

  returns **85 hits**, of which six are this docket's — the API matches fuzzily. Cite the six

</Callout>

## What the study uses instead, and what it costs

With no disclosed load and no disclosed floor area, the screen this network uses elsewhere has no
input. The IT load here is bracketed off the **one disclosed hard figure — the $3 billion campus
investment** — against a hyperscale critical-IT construction-cost band of roughly $8.5–20M per
MW-IT, an industry norm and `[reference]`, not a Sidney disclosure. That gives **150 MW low, ~353 MW
high, ~251 MW central**. `[inference]`

Two errors pull in opposite directions and roughly cancel: $3B spans land, shell and all phases, so
the screen runs high; the campus is multi-year, so near-term load runs low. What survives is an
order of magnitude, not a figure. **Every chapter that consumes it says so, and the moment an air
PTI or an interconnection filing names this campus's MW, it is replaced.**

## Cooling: one sentence on the whole public record

The cooling design is not disclosed, and this study keeps it `unknown` rather than choosing an
archetype. `[open]`

What exists is a single line in a City staff presentation heard by Council on 2026-04-27: *"All
wastewater, including **cooling tower** discharges, are required to be discharged to the sanitary
sewer."* `[verified]` — the only naming of the mechanism anywhere on the public record, and it comes
from the City, not the operator.

Against that, the contracted quantities read the other way. The water agreement reserves a **1.0 MGD**
peak withdrawal ceiling but projects **4.6 million gallons a year** — under **1.3%** of the ceiling
— of which 1.16M gal/yr returns to the sewer. `[verified]` A draw that small is consistent with a
largely closed-loop or dry design rather than an evaporative tower. `[inference]`

**Neither observation is a disclosure, and they do not agree.** The archetype stays unknown, and the
water chapter brackets rather than estimates — which is the correct treatment when the operator has
stated a *quantity* and withheld a *method*.

<Callout variant="takeaway">

  across four record systems, and the codename returns the wrong campus.
</Callout>
