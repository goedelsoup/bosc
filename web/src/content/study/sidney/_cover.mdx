Sidney's record is unusually complete for a campus this new. Eight council resolutions, four
executed agreements, a grading permit, five state permits and the approved minutes of every meeting
that voted on them are all in this corpus, most of them published by the City itself. What the
record is missing is not documents. It is **numbers the documents were never required to carry**.

Five state permits have issued or been filed for this campus and **not one states a load**. The
megawatt figure this study most needs exists — it is in an AES Ohio construction service agreement
filed with FERC — and it is filed **withheld**. So the IT load here is a screening bracket, and
every chapter that depends on it says so rather than borrowing a number from a press release.

The fiscal record inverts that. There the instruments are exact, and what they are exact about is
how little they bind. A thirty-year, hundred-percent abatement per building; a $46,000,000 PILOT
schedule to the dollar; an investment figure and a jobs figure that the same agreement states, in
terms, **cannot be compelled**; no clawback; and a company right to terminate on thirty days'
notice for any reason. The precision is real. It runs one way.

Two things this study can now say that it could not a week ago, both because the City's own
legislative portal turned out never to have been blocked. **The thirty-year exemption did not exist
until seven weeks before it was granted** — the Community Reinvestment Area's own ceiling was
fifteen years until September 2025. And **one of the six votes was not unanimous**: the agreement
splitting revenue with the school boards passed 5–2, which is invisible in the instrument because a
Sidney resolution page never records its own vote.

Every figure here is read from a cited record and tagged: `[verified]` when a document states it,
`[inference]` when it is derived and the assumption is shown, `[reference]` for secondary context,
`[open]` when the record does not say. Where a route was blocked — the county recorder's search, the
Power Siting Board's docket — that is written down as a fact about a server, re-tested, and dated.
Nothing is inferred from an access failure.

<Callout variant="open">
  **No member of the public is named in this study, and that is a limit of the record, not a
  choice.** The minutes report that "a number of people present raised questions and concerns" and
  that "those from the public speaking" objected — and they name and count nobody. Organized local
  opposition to this campus is reported in secondary sources. Until the meeting audio is produced,
  nothing here may be attributed to a person outside City government.
</Callout>
