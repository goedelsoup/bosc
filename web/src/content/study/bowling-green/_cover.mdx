Bowling Green is where this network's usual reading order breaks. At most sites the land record
comes first and the public decision follows it. Here the **end use was on the record nineteen months
before the operator's name was** — a county resolution in September 2023 recites that "Liames LLC
plans to construct one or more data center buildings," and Meta's name became public in 2025.

That inversion is why two of this study's chapters carry most of its weight. The **land assembly**
is a compiled county register, not a stack of deeds, so it can say who owns and cannot say who sold.
The **governance** record is a roll call that every published account reports backwards: a 2–1
in favour that the minutes record as 1–2 against a motion to adopt a denial, with the reported
dissenter the only trustee voting yes. Same outcome, inverted record.

The power side is a behind-the-meter gas plant certified with no adjudicatory hearing, and barred by
its own certificate from the grid it sits beside. The water side is a twelve-fold conflict between
two published figures that neither the withdrawal register nor the discharge permit can settle,
because both instruments are structurally blind to a campus buying finished municipal water.

Every figure here is read from a cited record and tagged: `[verified]` when a document states it,
`[inference]` when it is derived and the assumption is shown, `[reference]` for secondary context,
`[open]` when the record does not say. Where an access failure blocked a record — the Power Siting
Board's docket, the Secretary of State's business search — that is written down as a fact about a
server, and nothing is inferred from it.
