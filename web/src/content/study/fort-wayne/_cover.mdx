Fort Wayne is this network's first **out-of-state** watershed point — Allen County, **Indiana**,
not Lima's Allen County, Ohio — and its first **confirmed, operational** campus. That makes it the
clean comparison case: the same load-not-jobs subsidy shape, in a second jurisdiction, assembled
entirely from primary records.

A ~858-acre, $2 billion campus, locally codenamed **Project Zodiac**, is operating in southeast
Fort Wayne. The land was bought by a Delaware shell, Hatchworks LLC, for an operator the local
filings worked to keep at arm's length: Google. Three records carry most of this study — a
recorded deed chain, an IDEM Title V air permit, and a contested §401 water-quality certification.

The asymmetry between them is the finding. The air side is instrument-grade: a Title V permit,
34 gensets, an issued certification fought over in public. The water side is nearly empty — no
cooling method disclosed, no water-balance scenario computable, no discharge characterization
filed. **A campus can reach its operating permit with its entire water account off the record.**

Every figure here is read from a cited record and tagged for confidence: `[verified]` when a
document states it, `[inference]` when it is derived and the assumption is shown, `[reference]`
for secondary context, `[open]` when the record does not say. Where the record is silent this
study leaves it silent and names the ask — the opacity is the finding, not a gap to paper over.
