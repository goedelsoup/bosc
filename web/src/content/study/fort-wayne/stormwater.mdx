A data center drinks electricity and sheds water. This campus sits in the **Maumee headwaters**
(HUC 04100005), and to build it Google had to move a stream — which required a permit it had to
fight for.

## A contested certification

<Cite record="idem/fort-wayne/wqc001454.idem.yaml">IDEM issued the §401 water-quality certification
on 2026-06-05, over organized opposition</Cite> — a 90-page public-hearing transcript and ~295
pages of compiled comments, including from the Hoosier Environmental Council. `[verified]` That a
routine certification drew a contested hearing is itself the friction this platform exists to
document.

What it permits, in numbers, for Phase 3 alone:

- **Permanently relocates 3,415 linear feet of Adams Ditch** (0.78 ac, 3,794 cubic yards of fill)
  to clear the DC06 and DC07 footprints. `[verified]`
- **Fills 0.84 acre of forested Wetland 56**, to be restored as herbaceous wetland. `[verified]`
- Mitigates off-site: 160 lf of stream credits plus **2.52 acres of forested-wetland credits** at
  the Openings mitigation bank, and 3,255 lf of new on-site channel. `[verified]`

The receiving water is Adams Ditch → Doctor Ditch → the Maumee, and it is **distinct** from where
the Fort Wayne sewage plant discharges (IN0032191 → Baldwin Ditch). The campus sheds *stormwater*,
not sanitary effluent — commenters flagged the lost wetland's stormwater-filtering function
specifically. `[verified]`

## Reading the runoff off a drawing

A §401 covers the stream and wetland — the §404 nexus. It does not cover the runoff off the roofs
and the parking. For that you measure the plan. The §401's **Exhibit 7** is a scaled site plan
(1″ = 200′, "Alternative B"), and it can be measured: by colour-segmenting and scale-calibrating
the drawing, **~46 acres of building footprint** (seven data halls, ~290k sf each) plus **~180
acres of pavement** = **~226 acres impervious** on the 858-acre permit area, about **26%**.
`[inference]` Method uncertainty is roughly ±15–20%, and the firm regulatory figure awaits the
SWPPP / Rule 5 construction stormwater permit, still `[open]`.

Why it matters: the soils are hydrologic group **C drained, D undrained** — Pewamo and
Blount–Glynwood lake-plain clays that shed water fast. `[verified]` Two hundred-plus acres of new
impervious surface on high-runoff clay, draining to a relocated headwaters ditch, is a measurable
change to a small stream.

<Callout variant="takeaway">
  Lima's campus runs ~34% impervious against Fort Wayne's ~26% — the difference is wetland buffer
  the parcel keeps. Both figures are derived from a drawing rather than stated by a permit, which
  is why both carry `[inference]` and why the Rule 5 filing stays this chapter's ask.
</Callout>
