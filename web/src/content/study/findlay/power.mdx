The disclosed 150 megawatts comes with one more sentence from the same filing: "In 2024, we expanded
the Findlay Megawatt Hub to make available an additional **300 MW** of capacity with a standalone
interconnection site." `[verified]` Four hundred and fifty megawatts, in a county of 75,000 people,
from a company that withdrew the filing four months later.

## Start with the rule, not the reporting

The governing instrument is **AEP Ohio's Schedule DCT**, the data-center tariff, at P.U.C.O. No. 22,
Original Sheet Nos. 223-1 through 223-7. A great deal written about it is close to right. Four
things in the sheets bear directly on this site.

**It names cryptocurrency mining outright.** The "Mobile Data Center" definition reaches electronic
information services "(including mining of cryptocurrency)" with portable or distributable load
above **25,000 kW**. MARA's contracted 150,000 kW is six times that threshold. `[verified]`

**A Mobile Data Center customer must swear an oath** — "a sworn statement, under penalty of perjury,
that neither the customer nor any of its affiliates is owned or controlled by … any foreign
adversary as defined in 15 C.F.R. § 7.4." `[verified]` It is the only attestation anywhere in the
schedule.

**The +300 MW cannot ride a grandfather.** The schedule defines Existing Load that signs a new
service agreement to expand by more than 25,000 kW as *New Load*; 300 MW is twelve times that
trigger. `[inference]` — the conclusion follows from the sheets, but the premise, that an executed
pre-tariff agreement exists at all, is `[open]`.

**And the famous number is not what it is usually quoted as.** "Data centers must pay for 85% of
contracted capacity for 12 years" is too imprecise to quote. The 85% is a *floor*, applied during a
ramp (Year 1 50%, Year 2 65%, Year 3 80%, Year 4 90%) and afterwards to a rolling 11-month peak or a
Minimum Demand table. Existing Load bills at **60%**, not 85%. Term is the ramp plus eight.
`[verified]`

Read the tariff sheets, not the press summary. Every one of those four points circulates in
secondary coverage in a form that is subtly wrong — the threshold, the oath, the percentage, the
term. The sheets are public, they are seven pages, and they settle it.

## Who the customer is, though

Here the tariff runs out. One Power owns the site and its own transmission-voltage substation; MARA
leases capacity behind it. Whether Schedule DCT reaches the **host**, the **tenant**, or neither
turns on an electric service agreement nobody has produced. `[open]` That single unproduced document
would settle three questions at once — who the DCT customer is, whether the site is Existing or New
Load, and what it actually pays.
