Findlay does everything to one small river. It **impounds** the Blanchard into two upground
reservoirs, **augments** its low flow from them, **discharges** a 15-million-gallon-a-day sewage
plant into it at a point where the state's own arithmetic says there is no dilution left, and has
spent roughly **$160 million** trying to stop it from flooding downtown. Four miles north, behind a
township line that had no zoning at all until 2026, sits a **150-megawatt** contracted compute load.

Unlike Fort Wayne's Project Zodiac there is no developer codename to uncover here: the host and the
customer are both named in SEC filings, and the county's biggest employer is a Fortune 500 company
headquartered downtown. What is hidden in Findlay is not *who* — it is *how much*, *under whose
contract*, and *against what rule*. The load arrived first; the rules came after, from three
governments with three different reaches, and not one of them has an adopted instrument that reaches
the megawatts already contracted inside its borders.

This study also carries a retraction, and it is the most useful thing on it. The site was
commissioned on a finding — that the sewage plant runs over its phosphorus allocation every reported
year — that the record no longer supports. Rebuilding the load from the permit's own published
equation inverted it, and the honest answer to one of the three seasons is still *cannot be called*.
Saying so is not a weakness in the analysis; it is the analysis.

Every figure here is read from a cited record and tagged: `[verified]` when a document states it,
`[inference]` when it is derived and the assumption is shown, `[reference]` for a secondary source,
`[open]` when the record does not say. Several of Findlay's own instruments could not be retrieved
at all — the city's moratorium ordinance, the county's adopted SB 52 map, two county land systems.
Those are recorded as **access failures**, with their dates and response codes, and never as
findings of absence.

**One name collision governs this whole study.** MARA Holdings, Inc. (NASDAQ: MARA), the bitcoin
miner holding the 150 MW contract, is *not* Marathon Petroleum Corp (NYSE: MPC), the refiner
headquartered in Findlay. Two unrelated companies, both attachable to "Marathon … Findlay, Ohio."
They are never merged here.
