# Grid & consumer energy-cost reference — US EIA

Committed consumer energy-cost baseline for a site's corridor: what households in **that
site's state** pay for electricity and heating fuel, which utility and balancing authority
serve the campus, and the scale of the data-center load against those markets. The
consumer-price half of the demand thread (issue #91, from the 2026-06-10 facility-design
call: *"bring in fuel costs at the consumer level due to macro pressures and data-center
demand"*). Regenerated by `watermark eia` / `watermark grid`; the site/scenario read the
committed YAML (no live pull).

Every state-, utility- and BA-level value below is **resolved per site**, not baked in: the
reference build keeps the un-slugged `reference/eia/*.yaml`, and every other site's copies
live under `reference/eia/<slug>/`. A site in Indiana pulls the Indiana series and its own
serving utility; nothing about one site's grid is asserted of another's.

## The demand → consumer-price-pressure sensitivity

`watermark.economics.energy.derive_demand_pressure` links the facility's first-class total
**`facility_draw`** (`watermark.facility.power`, issue #87 — IT load × PUE) to this dataset:

- **Annual consumption** = facility draw × 8760 h × load factor (~0.9, assumption).
- **Demand share** = that consumption ÷ the site state's total retail electricity sales (EIA).
- **Households-equivalent** = consumption ÷ ~10,500 kWh/household/yr (EIA US avg).
- **Price-pressure band** = demand share × a stylized demand-to-price transmission
  coefficient (0.5–1.0 %price/%demand).

The demand share and households-equivalent are the **robust, EIA-cited headline**. The
price-pressure band is a **stylized screening sensitivity, not a forecast** — retail
price formation is far more complex than one coefficient.

## Known gaps & caveats

- **EIA requires a key** (`WATERMARK_EIA_API_KEY`, free). Without one, `watermark eia` replays the
  committed offline fixtures (`tests/fixtures/economics/eia/`); a live keyed pull
  refreshes to the latest annual vintage. Figures here are EIA **annual averages**
  (latest stable annual; EIA annual data lags ~1 year). The committed fixtures + figures
  are now a **genuine recorded keyed pull** — **2025 vintage** (`watermark eia`, #120),
  superseding the earlier hand-transcribed 2023 placeholders.
- **The campus buys at wholesale/industrial rates, not the residential price.** The
  residential price is the *consumer-impact reference*, not the campus's own bill.
- **Retail fuel is state-level natural gas, not gasoline.** EIA retail gasoline is
  PADD/region-level (Midwest PADD 2), not state; we use the site state's residential
  natural gas ($/Mcf) as the state-level consumer heating-fuel series and do not fabricate
  a county/state gasoline figure.
- Series values are **connector-pulled and regenerable**; EIA columns are selected by
  **name** — `period` plus the series' own value column (`price` / `sales` / `value` on
  the `/v2/seriesid` route), never by index.

### `grid-profile.yaml` specifics (issue #94)

- **The serving utility is grounded, never asserted.** Each `grid-profile.yaml` carries the
  utility with its own `source` and `confidence`: `document` where the site's record names a
  tariff outright, `reference` where the identification rests on the EIA-861 service-territory
  file plus the state commission's certified-territory map. A site whose serving LSE is not
  document-cited says so on the value rather than borrowing a neighbour's.
- **The RTO/BA is read off the utility**, not chosen — a utility in a PJM transmission zone
  is FERC-jurisdictional to PJM, and the profile records which BA applies to that site.
- **All three load denominators are connector-sourced (#94/#120).** Utility retail GWh +
  customers + avg price come from the **EIA-861 per-utility** "Sales to Ultimate Customers"
  bulk file (`watermark.grid.eia861`, 2024 vintage; downloaded to `econ_cache_dir/eia861/`, offline
  replays `tests/fixtures/economics/eia861/`); the BA's annual demand is the **EIA-930**
  daily-demand sum via `fetch_ba_annual_load`; the **state** retail denominator is shared
  with `consumer-energy.yaml` (#91).
- **A restructured utility reports in a split** — Bundled (SSO) + Delivery (shopping). Retail
  sales/customers are the **sum**, and the average price is the **bundled (full-service)
  revenue/sales**: delivery-only rows exclude generation, so a blended price would understate
  the all-in cost. This applies wherever the state's retail market is restructured.
- The campus load share derives from the first-class `facility_draw`
  (`watermark.facility.power`, issue #87 — IT load × PUE), at the same ~0.9 load factor as #91.

### `ba-interchange.yaml` specifics (issue #95)

- **EIA-930 region-data is hourly**; the connector **reduces it to window-mean
  aggregates inside the fetch** (demand mean/peak, net-generation mean, net-interchange
  mean/min/max, net-import-hours fraction) so the cached payload and committed fixture
  stay tiny. The window is a **representative summer month** (peak cooling demand,
  data-center relevant), not the full year — recorded in `period_start`/`period_end`.
- **Sign convention:** total interchange `TI` is **+ for net exports, − for net
  imports** (EIA-930). One BA window is committed today — **PJM**, which is a net exporter
  on average and is the balancing authority every site registered so far resolves to. A
  site outside PJM pulls its own BA rather than reading this one.
- Keyed (`WATERMARK_EIA_API_KEY`); offline replays `tests/fixtures/economics/eia930/`. The
  committed fixture + figures are a **genuine recorded keyed pull** of the **2024-06**
  window (`watermark interchange`, #95/#120), superseding the earlier hand-authored aggregates
  (which had overstated net-import hours as ~24% vs the real ~0.6%).
- The derived `CampusInterchangeComparison` is a **screening** view over window means,
  **not** an hourly dispatch or locational (LMP/congestion) model — deliverability to the
  campus bus is the RTO interconnection-queue layer (#96).
