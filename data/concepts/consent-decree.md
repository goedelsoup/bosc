A **consent decree** is a settlement that a court adopts as its own order. Rather than
litigate to judgment, the parties negotiate terms — often injunctive steps, a
compliance schedule, and penalties — and the court enters them, so the agreement
becomes judicially enforceable and stays under the court's supervision until its
obligations are met.

In the environmental record the consent decree is the usual endpoint of a serious
enforcement track: a discharger in [[significant non-compliance]], or a party liable
under [[CERCLA]], resolves the matter by agreeing to a decree that fixes what it must
do and by when. Because it is a public court document, a consent decree is a
high-value, [verified] source — it states the violations conceded, the remedy imposed,
and the deadlines that follow, and it often surfaces in [[ECHO]] as the formal action
that closed out a compliance case.
