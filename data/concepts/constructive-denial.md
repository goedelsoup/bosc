---
title: Constructive denial
kind: concept
aliases: [constructive denial of records]
tags: [records, legal]
summary: An agency's failure to produce public records within a reasonable time, treated in law as a denial even though no formal refusal was issued.
related: [public-records-mandamus, orc]
---

**Constructive denial** is the doctrine that an agency can deny a public-records
request without ever saying no. Under Ohio's Public Records Act (R.C. 149.43), records
must be produced within a *reasonable* time; when an agency instead lets a request sit,
runs out the clock, or answers with open-ended delay, the law can treat that silence as
a denial the requester may act on.

The concept matters because it closes an obvious loophole: if only an explicit refusal
were reviewable, an agency could defeat transparency simply by not responding.
Constructive denial gives the requester a ripened claim — the predicate for a
[[public-records mandamus]] action to compel production. In this record, where the
requester seeks documents an agency is slow to release, the timing and content of the
agency's non-response is what establishes whether a denial has constructively occurred
under the [[Ohio Revised Code]].
