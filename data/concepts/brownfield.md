A **brownfield** is a property — usually a former industrial or commercial site —
whose reuse is complicated by the presence, or the suspicion, of contamination. The
label is regulatory shorthand that unlocks assessment and cleanup programs and
dedicated grants, on the theory that redeveloping an already-disturbed site is
preferable to consuming greenfield land.

A brownfield sits at the seam of the environmental and land-use threads. Its
contamination history may tie to a formal [[CERCLA]] liability or to a lesser state
cleanup, and its redevelopment is frequently sweetened with a [[tax abatement]] on top
of remediation funding. For a facility record, the brownfield designation is both a
constraint and a lead: it flags residual contamination that governs what can be built
and how, and it points back to the site's prior uses and the parties responsible for
them.
