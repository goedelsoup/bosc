A **regional transmission organization** is an independent, FERC-regulated entity
that operates the high-voltage grid and wholesale electricity market for a region,
keeping the transmission system neutral among the utilities and generators that use
it. An *independent system operator* (ISO) is the functionally equivalent term; the
label differs by region but the role is the same.

The RTO is the level at which grid capacity is actually allocated, through the
[[interconnection queue]] and capacity market it administers. For this record the RTO
is [[PJM]], and its rules — set under [[FERC]] tariffs — determine how quickly a new
hyperscale load or a behind-the-meter plant can connect, and what it must pay to do
so. Understanding the RTO's process is what turns a vague "grid constraint" into a
specific, dated position in a queue.
