The **runoff curve number** is a single coefficient between 0 and 100 that captures
how much of a rainfall a piece of land turns into runoff. A low CN (forest on sandy
[[HSG]] A soil) absorbs most of a storm; a high CN (pavement, or clay HSG D soil)
sheds nearly all of it. The NRCS method looks the number up from a land-cover ×
soil-group table.

Curve number is where land-use change becomes hydrology. Converting cropland or
woods to the roofs and parking of a large campus raises the site CN, so the same
storm yields more runoff, faster — the effect a post-construction stormwater design
and its [[erosion and sediment control]] have to hold. Because the output is
sensitive to the CN, a small error in the assumed soil group or cover class
propagates straight into the modeled peak flow.
