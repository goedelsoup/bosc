The **Ohio Power Siting Board** is the state body that must certify major energy
infrastructure — large generating plants, high-voltage transmission lines, and big
substations — before they are built. Its review weighs need, environmental impact,
and public comment through an evidentiary case docketed under a case number, which
makes the OPSB file a rich public record of a project's rationale and opposition.

For the data-center thread the OPSB is often where the *power* side of a campus first
surfaces publicly. A new substation or a behind-the-meter generation plant sized to a
hyperscale load lands on the board's docket, and the filing can reveal the load —
and the operator — before any economic-development announcement does. The board is
distinct from the [[PUCO]], which regulates utility service and rates, and from the
federal [[FERC]]; the OPSB decides *where* big energy facilities go.
