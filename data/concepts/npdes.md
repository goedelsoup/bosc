The **National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System** is the Clean Water Act
program that licenses point-source discharges to waters of the United States. An
NPDES permit names the outfall and receiving water, caps each regulated pollutant
with numeric [[effluent]] limits, and sets the monitoring and reporting the
permittee must file — chiefly the [[discharge monitoring report]].

In Ohio the program is delegated to the [[Ohio EPA]], which issues NPDES permits
under state authority; a new or expanded source also needs a [[permit to install]]
before construction. The permit's fact sheet is the platform's primary source for a
receiving water's design low flow (the [[7Q10]]) and the [[assimilative capacity]]
available to dilute a load — the same denominators that a large new
[[consumptive cooling]] withdrawal would draw down.

Where a receiving water is impaired, the permit's limits are meant to implement the
water body's [[total maximum daily load]] through a point-source
[[wasteload allocation]]. Compliance history — inspections, violations, and any
[[significant non-compliance]] flag — is public through EPA's [[ECHO]].
