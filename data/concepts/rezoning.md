A **rezoning** is a legislative act that changes the zoning classification of a
parcel — an industrial district applied to former farmland, say — so a use the old
zoning barred becomes permitted. Because it is legislative, it is usually decided by
the elected body after a planning-commission recommendation and public hearing, and it
is the approval most exposed to organized opposition and, in some jurisdictions, to
referendum.

Rezoning is usually the hinge in a data-center land record: the campus needs
industrial or a special district, and the vote to grant it is where a community's
position becomes official and, depending on the jurisdiction, open to referendum or
judicial challenge. It is distinct from the lesser approvals a
project might instead seek — a [[conditional use]] permit within the existing zoning,
or a [[variance]] from a single standard — and it is the approval a
[[moratorium]] most often pauses. Tracking the rezoning vote, its conditions, and any
referendum is how the platform fixes when and on what terms a project was actually
authorized.
