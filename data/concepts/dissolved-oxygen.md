**Dissolved oxygen** is the concentration of oxygen dissolved in water, the supply
that fish and aquatic insects breathe. It is a foundational water-quality criterion:
a stream must hold dissolved oxygen above a numeric floor, and an [[NPDES]] permit's
[[effluent]] limits are set partly to protect it.

Dissolved oxygen is where several loads converge. Oxygen-demanding organic matter
and ammonia in a discharge consume it directly; nutrient loading consumes it
indirectly, because the algal blooms that [[total phosphorus]] and
[[total nitrogen]] feed die and decay, drawing the reach down into an oxygen sag.
Low flow makes all of this worse — less water holds less oxygen and dilutes less
load — which is why a discharge's oxygen impact is judged at the same design low
flow as its [[dilution]].
