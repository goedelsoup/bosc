A **moratorium** is a time-limited pause a local government imposes on accepting or
approving a category of development — commonly while it drafts or revises zoning to
catch up with a pressure it did not anticipate. It freezes the status quo so
applications cannot vest under rules the community has decided to change.

In this record the moratorium is a recurring civic response to data-center demand: a
township or city facing a rush of large-load proposals halts approvals to write siting
standards, buffer or water rules, or an outright district. A moratorium is a live
signal — it marks where a community is contesting a project rather than waving it
through — and its timing relative to a specific application often determines whether
that project proceeds under old rules or new ones. It typically travels with a
[[rezoning]] fight, since the rules being paused are the ones a rezoning would apply.
