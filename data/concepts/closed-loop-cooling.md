**Closed-loop cooling** recirculates its cooling water rather than returning it to
the source. The system loses water two ways — evaporation from the cooling tower, and
periodic *blowdown* to purge concentrated minerals — and makes up the difference with
fresh withdrawal. The result inverts the once-through profile: a modest withdrawal
but a high consumptive fraction.

For a [[hyperscale data center]] this is the design that matters to the river,
because most of what it withdraws is lost to evaporation and never returns
downstream — the [[consumptive cooling]] that is subtracted from live flow in the
[[dilution]] and [[assimilative capacity]] screen. It also concentrates whatever is
in the make-up water, so blowdown itself can become a permitted discharge. A
recirculating design cuts the withdrawal an [[once-through cooling]] plant would need
while raising the share that leaves the watershed for good.
