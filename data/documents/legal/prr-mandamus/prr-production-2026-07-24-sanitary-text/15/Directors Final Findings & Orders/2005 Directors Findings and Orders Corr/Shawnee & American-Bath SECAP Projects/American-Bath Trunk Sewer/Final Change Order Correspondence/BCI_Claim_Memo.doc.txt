ALLEN COUNTY
AMERICAN BATH TRUNK SEWER PROJECT

April 28, 2010

Beaverdam Contracting, Inc. Claim #1

In evaluation of the claim submitted by Beaverdam Contracting, Inc., the following items were reviewed for additional payment on the American Bath Trunk Sewer Project as provided by BCI:

    1. Additional labor and equipment hours
    2. Additional asphalt and aggregate
    3. Additional traffic control measures

The following items have been submitted for review:

    1. BCI letter dated March 8, 2010
    2. BCI letter dated March 30, 2010
    3. BCI letter dated April 8, 2010

The claim was for performance of work on the Cole Street portion of the project.  This work was performed between June 9, 2009 and November 12, 2009.  Furthermore, this work included Manhole 8 through Manhole 17, and 2,912 L.F. of 24” sewer with compacted granular backfill between Stations 19+66 and 48+78.

The trench width was determined by referencing pictures taken during construction and conversations with the County inspector.  Per a site visit by Jim Lee, he recalls the trench being 10 feet wide for 1,083’ of alignment near the Church, and 13 feet wide for 80’ of the alignment at Station 29+95.  The trench on the rest of the alignment was between 6-8’ wide.  The average trench width along Cole Street was determined to be 8 feet, which was derived from the above information.

The last piece of information used to evaluate the claim was what the trench width should have been assumed to be by the Contractor prior to construction and during estimating of the bid prices.  The Contractor stated that he assumed a 4 foot width but this is not practical given the depth and dimensions of the trench box.  Per conversations with other Contractors and construction managers, it was determined that the trench width should have been assumed to be approximately 6 feet in width due to the depths of up to 25’ for estimating purposes.  A trench width of 6 feet will be used to evaluate the pavement and aggregate issues since this should have been an assumed approximation of the trench width due to the depth of the sewers.

1.  Additional Labor and Equipment Hours

BCI and Allen County’s inspection reports were tabulated and contain the total labor and equipment hours performed on the Cole Street alignment.

BCI Reports Total Labor and Equipment = $647,012.88

Allen County Inspection Reports Total Labor and Equipment = $369,749.00

Original Bid Price for Labor and Equipment = $218,840.00

Therefore, the Total Overage for Labor and Equipment for Consideration =

$369,749.00 - $218,840.00 = $150,909.00

2.  Additional Asphalt and Aggregate

Stone Quantities:

BCI estimated 13,500 Tons of aggregate backfill and used nearly 21,000 Tons, according to the letter dated March 30, 2010.  The stone tickets were calculated and a summary spreadsheet is attached which verifies this amount.  We calculated the total amount of aggregate that should have been estimated using the assumed 6 foot trench width, and the total came out to be 22,646 Tons for the Cole Street alignment.  The decision was made to allow the blended material of #57’s and sand to reduce the overall cost associated with the widening trench.  The total quantities installed are lower than what BCI should have estimated therefore no additional payment is required for the stone quantities.

Total Overage = $0.00

Asphalt Quantities:

BCI requested the following quantities and unit prices for asphalt on Cole Street:

Asphalt Base	505 C.Y.* 	$128.00/C.Y.		$64,640.00
Overlay	319 C.Y.*	$169.00/C.Y.		$53,911.00
							$118,551.00

*These quantities do not include the asphalt for Knollwood.

With an assumed 6 foot trench width, the total amount of asphalt that should have been estimated was calculated to be 606 C.Y. for the Cole Street alignment.

The bid price for Type 1 Asphalt Pavement was $36,492.00

Therefore, the price per C.Y. during the bid was $60.21/C.Y.

BCI’s total quantity installed on Cole Street was 824 C.Y.

Therefore, 824 C.Y. * $60.21/C.Y. = $49,613.04

Total Overage = $49,613.04 - $36,492.00 = $13,121.04

3.  Additional Traffic Control Measures

BCI’s claim for traffic control is $23,818.00

The price quote from A&A Safety was $10,960.00 for the period between 11/1/2009 and 3/23/2010, for a total of 210 days.  The total for signage during this period was $52.19 per day.  BCI’s claim includes 143 additional days of signage, therefore the total for this period is:  143 Days * $52.19/Day = $7,463.24

The bid price for traffic control was $15,000.00

Therefore, the total overage = $10,960.00 + $7,463.24 - $15,000.00 = $3,423.24

Final Claim Amount:

Labor and Equipment = $150,909.00

Aggregate = $0.00

Asphalt = $13,121.04

Traffic Control = $3,423.24

Total = $167,453.28
