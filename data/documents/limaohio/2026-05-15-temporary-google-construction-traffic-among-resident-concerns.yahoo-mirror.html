Temporary Google construction traffic among resident concerns

Peter Bonasso, The Lima News, Ohio
Fri, May 15, 2026 at 2:31 PM UTC
5 min read
Add Yahoo as a preferred source to see more of our stories on Google.
  • The Port Authority of Allen County is constructing roundabouts at key intersections funded by Google to improve vehicle access in anticipation of increased traffic due to the upcoming data center.
  • Construction on the state Route 115 roundabout will begin on June 1, followed by the Bluelick Road roundabout in August, with efforts to address traffic congestion and road repairs to counteract construction vehicle damage.
  • Concerned residents in the area feel that the roundabouts are more beneficial for Google than the local community, expressing frustrations over transparency and lack of input in meetings regarding the data center project.

LIMA — The Port Authority of Allen County was met with concerned residents at its open house explaining the North Cole Street Improvement Project on Thursday at the Allen County Sanitary Engineering building.

The project, which is funded by Google, includes the construction of roundabouts at the intersections of North Cole Street and state Route 115, Beery Road and Bluelick Road, as well as other improvements to the affected roads for improved vehicle access. The construction efforts arrive in the light of Google's data center advancing in the design process, as they will alleviate the increased traffic expected to and from the center.

However, residents in the area said they felt the good of the community isn't being fully taken into consideration.

The state Route 115 roundabout will be the first to undergo construction on June 1, with the Bluelick Road one to follow in August and the Beery Road construction start date yet to be determined. Theresa Pollick, a port authority communications officer, said the roundabouts address traffic congestion in the midst of and before the data center's arrival.

"We know that there's a lot of people that commute down to Lima or other destinations. It's obviously I-75 trying to get to US-30; I mean, these are high-traffic areas," Pollick said. "If we make these improvements, it is going to help people get around."

Allen County Engineer Brion Rhodes is also planning for road repair efforts to counteract the toll of construction vehicles. While he focused on Bluelick Road, the engineer could envision repaving of Cole Street as well, depending on the damage it sustains.

"Bluelick between Cole Street and Route 65 is a township road, and that is not as sturdy of a roadway as what Cole Street is," Rhodes said. "So I'm expecting that might receive some more damage, and we'll have to do some repairs. And so when we do those repairs, we'll likely do some widening as well."

Residents in the area, however, said the roundabouts are more for the benefit of Google than the local community. Lynne Focht, a Sugar Creek Township resident, is a co-owner of property near the state Route 115 intersection, whose part of the land will cede a permanent easement to the proposed roundabout. She said the land would be used for construction traffic, but once that's over, she still will "never own that land again."

Cindy Leis, executive director of the port authority, said real estate agents have contacted all property owners whose land would be affected by the right-of-way easements.

David Streeter, also in Sugar Creek Township, echoed concern for the big development with temporary construction.

"This is all about Google trying to address the traffic issue that Google is creating with construction. That's all this is, is to address construction traffic," Streeter said. "None of it has to do with long-term health to the individuals, to the residents."

Andrew Schetter, a design engineer specializing in the Beery Road roundabout, spoke about that intersection's development as the port authority members tried to mitigate resident impact.

"We were originally looking at doing work down Beery all the way to Pike Run, and we were looking at that like, OK, there's just a lot of maintenance and a lot of impacts to people," Schetter said. "Now, when it came down to it, we were going for least impact. That set us back a little bit, but I think the extra time has been worth it to come up with something better."

The residents said their unifying concern with the project is transparency of what everything in the project is intended for. Focht voiced other Sugar Creek and American township residents' frustrations at not being heard at meetings regarding the data center in the first place. However, she said residents having a contact person at Google with whom to discuss the project would go a long way toward improving their sentiments.

Pollick said that detours and road closures relating to the state Route 115 roundabout will be announced in the coming days. For more updates on the project, visit allencoport.org.

Design developments

The Allen County commissioners agreed to enter into contract with Peterson Construction Company for construction manager at risk pre-construction design services with the infrastructure for the upcoming data center at their Thursday meeting.

The Allen County Engineer's Office hired an engineer from Peterson Construction six months ago, who has gotten the data center design to 30 to 40% completion, said Allen County Sanitary Engineer Brad Niemeyer. The company will now work with Allen County and Google in the pre-construction phase to get early procurements for items with long wait times, such as the generator and electronic components, as well as to improve the center's design.

Once the team gets to 60 to 70% of the final design, Peterson Construction will give its guaranteed maximum price for the project's construction. Allen County will then have the choice to either accept the company for construction with that maximum price allowance or look elsewhere.

The data center's pump station and forcemain will be the next elements that the team is looking at for construction, Niemeyer said. If Peterson Company is hired, the team would get to work on those parts in the fall.

The projected completion of the data center would be around January to March of 2028, according to Niemeyer.
