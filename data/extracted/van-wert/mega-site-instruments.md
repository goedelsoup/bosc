# Van Wert Mega Site — the primary-instrument set

Digest of [`mega-site-instruments.yaml`](mega-site-instruments.yaml), the extraction of the nine
City of Van Wert documents ingested under
[`data/documents/van-wert/council/`](../../documents/van-wert/council/) for **#1401**. Every
figure below is cited to a file and a 0-based PDF page; the YAML carries the full record,
including the parts of it that contradict each other.

Tags are BOSC evidentiary discipline: `[verified]` = stated in a captured primary record,
`[reference]` = secondary source, `[inference]` = reasoned, arithmetic shown, `[open]` = not
established.

## What changed

Before this pass the Van Wert corpus held the *water* instruments (#1406) and the *land* geometry
(#1403). The campus's legal existence — the annexation, the zoning, and the code amendment that
made a data center a permitted use in Van Wert at all — was carried on newspaper citations. It
now rests on the City's own record.

## 1 — A 2026 ordinance reaches back to 2014 to justify itself

Ordinance 26-05-028 declares itself "in furtherance of" **three Pre-Annexation Agreements**, each
recited as being among City Council, a named township's board of trustees, and the Board of County
Commissioners. `[verified]` (`26-05-028-draft-ordinance.pdf` p. 1)

| Date | Township |
|---|---|
| **2014-11-14** | Ridge |
| **2014-12-22** | Hoaglin |
| **2016-06-14** | Pleasant |

**That recital is the whole of what is verified** — the dates, the parties, and the ordinance's own
statement of what it furthers. None of the three agreements is in the corpus. Their terms, the
territory they cover, whether they bind anyone to anything, and whether they bear on this
annexation beyond being cited by it are all `[open]`.

`[inference]` Reading them as a decade-early runway for the campus is the obvious reading, and it
is only that: an inference from a recital, resting on documents nobody in this project has seen.
It should not be restated as fact, and nothing here supports saying the jurisdictions had "already
agreed" to this project — the agreements predate it by a decade and may have nothing to do with a
data center. What is solid, and was invisible from the press coverage the register previously
cited, is that the annexation reached back to 2014 and 2016 instruments to justify itself. That is
why those three agreements are the priority-1 pull.

The recurring public-comment claim that the site has been "designated and pre-annexed for
industrial use **since 2007**" is **not supported by anything in this instrument set**. `[open]`
Carry the three agreement dates, not 2007.

## 2 — The City defined "data center" and zoned 901.698 acres for it in the same sitting

Ordinance **26-05-029** wrote the term into the Van Wert Code of Ordinances for the first time.
`[verified]` (`26-05-029.pdf` pp. 2-3, Exhibit A):

> "**Data Center.** A facility comprised of a building or group of buildings designed to
> incorporate a large number of computer servers used for the storage of, processing, and
> distribution of large amounts of data, and the accessory equipment and structures necessary to
> support the critical functions of the facility such as (but not limited to) **generators,
> cooling towers, fuel storage, and electrical equipment**."

The same ordinance appended `Data Center` to the I-2 permitted-use list and rewrote "Public
Service Facility" so that **power plants and substations can only operate in I-2**. Ordinance
**26-05-030** then designated 901.698 ± acres I-2 under a §150.12(C) conditional zoning petition.

The definition is generic and would permit an evaporative build; it does not contradict this
campus's closed-loop disclosures, and it is not a constraint on them. It is what binds the next
applicant.

### The "conditional" in the conditional zoning is a landscape mound

**Exhibit C is the entire set of conditions** attached to the I-2 designation `[verified]`
(`26-05-030.pdf` p. 11): a minimum six-foot mound with a mixed landscape buffer reaching 75%
opacity five years after planting to 12 feet, required **only** where a parking area, structure,
building or permanent equipment sits within **1,000 linear feet** of a residentially used or zoned
abutter, installed by the earlier of 60 days after paving or before vertical construction.

No noise limit. No height limit. No water-supply or discharge condition. No hours-of-construction
limit. No setback beyond the zoning minimum. That is a **recorded absence** in the instrument —
the City may hold such conditions elsewhere, and none is in the corpus.

## 3 — The acreage, reconciled, and two register corrections

Exhibit A's four component acreages sum **exactly** to the acreage in both ordinance titles.
`[verified]`

```text
Parcel One   630.491 (Sec. 6, Ridge) + 114.861 (Ridge) + 31.213 (Sec. 31, Hoaglin) = 776.565 ac
Parcel Two                                          125.133 ac (Sec. 1, Pleasant)
Total                                               901.698 ac  ← the title
```

Two things the register carried from press coverage are now corrected against the instruments:

- **Not ~962 acres — 901.698 ± acres.** The 962 figure has no support in the record.
- **Not "6-0."** The minutes record **no numeric tally** for any of the three ordinances; each
  reads *"all concurred. Ordinance passed."* Seven members answered roll call and Roberts was
  excused during public comment, abstaining as **an employee of the Marsh Foundation**, leaving
  six. The one roll-called Mega Site vote in the set is the 2026-04-27 preparation motion:
  *"Hurless, Agler, Ringwald, Johnson, Block, and Moore voted yes. Roberts abstained."*
  (`4.27.26.pdf` p. 2)

The zoned area agrees with the #1403 deeded geometry to within 1.1 acre (901.698 ± vs 900.59
deeded / 901.502 planar). The **parcel list does not**. The hearing notice — the notice mailed to
adjoining landowners — names **four** parcels (`17-034718.0000`, `33-047500.0000`,
`12-034459.0000`, `17-034718.0100`), which against the committed CAMA acreages total **742.75 ac**,
or **82.4%** of what the ordinance zones. The missing **157.84 ac** is `17-034718.0200`, the fifth
deeded parcel, unnamed in the notice. `[inference]` The likeliest explanation is timing: the
petition was filed 2026-03-09, and the county survey records on these parcels (`VW-SD522-1/-2/-523`,
`VW-SD524`) are the splits that carved the campus out of the Marsh tract, so `.0200` may not have
existed as a number then. Not established — the Exhibit A metes-and-bounds, not the parcel list,
defines the zoned area, and reconciling the two is the cleanest remaining check on the boundary.

## 4 — The record contradicts itself on its own statutory route

| Says **Type 2** | Says **Type 1** |
|---|---|
| The committee motion that ordered it, 2026-04-27 (`4.27.26.pdf` p. 3) | The title as read at passage, 2026-05-11 (`5.11.26.pdf` p. 3) |
| The ordinance title the City posted 2026-05-07 (`26-05-028-draft-ordinance.pdf` p. 0) | |
| The 2026-05-11 agenda, before amendment (`5.11.26.pdf` p. 0) | |

The City's explanation, from the top of the 2026-05-11 meeting: a "typographical error **in the
agenda only**, the Ordinance title is true and accurate." The minutes then add: *"This EXPEDITED
TYPE 1 ANNEXATION is consistent with the change in the Agenda at the start of the meeting and is
consistent with the heading of Ordinance 26-05-028 itself."*

**It is not consistent with that heading.** The ordinance the City published on 2026-05-07 is
titled `AN ORDINANCE TO ACCEPT THE EXPEDITED TYPE 2 ANNEXATION OF 901.698+/- ACRES…`, and the
committee motion two weeks earlier said Type 2 as well. Both readings were confirmed **visually**
at 400 DPI, so this is a discrepancy in the record, not an OCR artifact.

It cannot be settled from this corpus, because **no signed or certified copy of any of the three
ordinances is public.** All six ordinance PDFs were uploaded 2026-05-07 at 17:10 UTC — four days
*before* the vote — and each prints an unfilled `Passed this ___ day of ___, 2026`. `[verified]`

R.C. 709.022 (expedited type 1) and R.C. 709.023 (expedited type 2) are different procedures with
different consequences for township taxation. `[inference]` The three recited Pre-Annexation
Agreements are the kind of instrument a type-1 route rests on — but that is a reading of the
statute against the recitals, not a fact the record states. **A certified copy from the Clerk of
Council is the resolution route, and it is the top of the pull list.**

## 5 — Water: the City's own record disagrees with itself

Three volumes for one initial fill, all on the 2026-05-04 hearing record `[verified]`:

| Volume | Framing | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **~660,000 gal** | "a **one-time** transaction required only at startup"; "the city will sell the developers" | Jay C. Fleming, Safety-Service Director, City press release 2026-04-21 (p. 14) |
| **700,000 gal** | "less than a half a day's water … **that will last for 6 to 8 years**" | Thad Lichtensteiger, Van Wert County Commissioner, written comment (p. 15) |
| **5,500 gal** | attributed to the city-launched project website | written public comment (p. 104) |

Two officials a week apart give two volumes, and they do not agree on whether the purchase
recurs: "one-time" against "will last for 6 to 8 years," which describes a service life.

And six days after the press release, the same Safety-Service Director told Council the City
**could not** supply fill water at all — *"no, even if the question presented itself, the city
would be incapable of fulfilling the requires"* (`4.27.26.pdf` p. 4). Both statements are in the
record; neither is reconciled here.

This is the **#1409** fill-vs-annual ambiguity that B2 (#1682) carries as
`WaterAccount.disclosed_makeup`. The primary record **deepens** it rather than settling it — the
conflict is not between the corpus and the City, it is inside the City's own record. Per the B2
rule a self-report never upgrades the source, so the closed-loop pin stays `[reference]` and the
gap stays a gap.

Fleming also stated that facility water sent for cleaning or disposal *"goes directly to the
wastewater treatment plant … It will not be put in the ground, aquifers or town creek."*
`[verified]` **Van Wert's WWTP discharges to Town Creek** — outfall 001 at River Mile 13.87 on
COMID 15653063, the same flowline as the City's intake (#1406). The statement is defensible about
*direct* discharge and about groundwater; as stated it is not accurate about Town Creek.
`[inference]` If the campus routes blowdown to the WWTP, the receiving-water question becomes what
it adds to a plant already under a dated CSO construction schedule — a different and more
tractable question than the one the public debate was having.

## 6 — Grid: AEP Ohio's own pipeline, on a municipal record

Zach Miller, Director of Economic Development and Data Center Integration, AEP Ohio, testifying
2026-05-04 `[verified]` (`5.4.26-Public-Hearing-1.pdf` pp. 11-12):

> "Early interest totaled more than **30,000 megawatts** of potential demand. Today, projects
> estimating **5,342 MW** have advanced to signed agreements."

~30 GW of inquiry against 5,342 MW under signed agreement — an 18% conversion, stated by a utility
officer on a municipal public record. It is the first primary-source AEP Ohio pipeline figure in
this corpus.

The testimony states **no megawatt figure for the Van Wert campus**, so the 500 MW bracket on the
Van Wert `SiteProfile` (#1402) stays `[reference]` and is not upgraded by it. The tariff Miller
describes as "approved … last summer" matches PUCO **24-508-EL-ATA** (approved 2025-07-09); the
case number is not printed and the identification is ours. `[inference]`

Separately, Commissioner Lichtensteiger's written comment puts Thor's power-upgrade cost at
**"$72 million"** `[reference]` — unsourced, and not the **$45M** the register carries for OPSB
**25-0697-EL-BLN** (the Van Wert–Haviland 138 kV line). The two may cover different scopes;
neither is reconciled.

## 7 — 21 of 88 written-comment pages share an identical opening

Of the 88 written-comment pages in the 2026-05-04 record, **21 share an identical normalised
900-character opening with an earlier page**. Clustering on that prefix gives **67 distinct
openings across 88 pages**, with 15 clusters holding more than one page and the largest holding
**five**. `[verified]` — reproducible from the committed PDF.

**They are near-identical, not verbatim, and the distinction is measured.** Compared over the
*whole* normalised body, only **one** pair is byte-identical (pp. 17 and 64, two scans of the same
two-page letter) — 87 distinct bodies across 88 pages on that test. The other 20 diverge somewhere
after the first 900 characters, but only slightly: full-body similarity to their cluster's first
page runs **0.974–1.000, median 0.987** (`difflib.SequenceMatcher` over normalised text, median
body 1,114 characters), and the divergence sits in the tail — signature blocks, addresses and
closing lines, which are handwritten and OCR-noisy. `[verified]`

The count is of **pages, not people**: a cluster may be one letter scanned twice, a two-page letter
counted twice, or different signers submitting the same text. Who signed what is not established
from this file.

`[inference]` Twenty-one pages opening with 900 identical characters and staying ~98–99% identical
thereafter is very hard to produce by independent authorship, and the same claim set recurs across
them in near-identical wording — $10 billion invested, **$200 million in community revenue over 20
years**, **200-250 permanent jobs at roughly $80,000**, **1,000-1,500 construction jobs**,
closed-loop cooling, and the unsupported 2007 date. Reading that as a coordinated letter campaign
is the natural inference and is labelled as one; who organised it, whether signers knew the text
was shared, and how many distinct people are behind the 21 pages are all `[open]`. What is
independent of the inference: **none of those figures appears in any instrument**, so they are
`[reference]` at best wherever they are repeated.

One written comment (pp. 104-106) argues the water case with a full reference list, and two of its
figures — the **1.01 billion gallon** reservoir and the **180 MG–1.26 BG** annual creek yield —
are the same figures independently committed at
[`water-watch.yaml`](water-watch.yaml) from the City's own utilities publications. Its arithmetic
elsewhere does not survive checking (it conflates MW with MWh). It also cites Ohio EPA's draft
data-center general permit **OHD000001** as live; Ohio EPA **withdrew** that draft on 2026-07-21
(#1685), so the hearing record preserves a regulatory premise that has since lapsed.

## 8 — Two things the same council did the same night

Ordinance **26-05-031**, also an emergency, moved **$700,000** from the sewer fund to cover a CSO
project bid of **$2.1M against a $1.85M estimate** — the **only** bid, which the City was
"obligated to move forward under Ohio EPA guidelines." `[verified]` (`5.11.26.pdf` pp. 0, 2)

NPDES **2PD00006\*WD** put the CSO Long Term Control Plan on a dated construction schedule whose
first milestone — begin construction, event code 3099 — was due **2026-08-01** (#1406). This is
that money, appropriated eleven weeks before the deadline, at a 14% overrun on a single-bid
procurement. The annexation and the CSO milestone were carried by the same council on the same
evening.

## 9 — Who is, and is not, in the instruments

The annexation petitioners are named on the record by the City Auditor: **The Marsh Foundation
and Van Wert East Owner LLC**, with **Charles F. Koch** as agent for petitioners. `[verified]`
(`4.27.26.pdf` p. 0; `5.11.26.pdf` p. 1) That is this corpus's first primary-source naming of the
Thor SPE as a party to a Van Wert instrument — #1403 found it gone from the county roll by
2026-07-31; here it is as a petitioner five months earlier.

**QTS is named nowhere in any of the nine documents.** `[open]` The City legislated the campus
without the end user appearing in the record; VWAED's letter says only that Thor "successfully
identified an end user."

Also on the record: **Thor Equities sponsored** the 2026-04-20 stakeholder tour of the New Albany
data-center campus, thanked in a letter read on behalf of Council (`4.27.26.pdf` p. 1); **VWAED
Executive Director Brent Stevens** "first sourced this opportunity more than two years ago" after
vetting "over 25 different data center groups," and urged Council to approve "the necessary
**incentives**, zoning, and permitting" (pp. 23-24) — the only appearance of the word in the set,
against **no Mega Site incentive instrument in the corpus** (#1407).

## 10 — Per-source negatives from this pass

- **PUCO/OPSB DIS** — both `25-0697-EL-BLN` and `24-508-EL-ATA` returned the 245-byte F5
  "Request Rejected" bot challenge. Third confirmation of this block (#1437, #1464).
- **Van Wert County Recorder (GovOS CountyFusion)** — reachable, but a session-based Java login,
  not a document index. The deed chain remains uningested.
- **`vanwertohiodatacenter.com`** — **the chain-of-custody window flagged in #1401 has already
  closed.** The live domain 301-redirects to `q.com/data-centers/van-wert`. The Internet Archive
  holds captures of `/` and `/answers`, but the site was a client-rendered Next.js application:
  the archived `/answers` HTML renders `Loading topics…` and no FAQ prose. The two `/answers`
  captures have different digests, so the page **did** change between 2026-04-01 and 2026-04-13 —
  but neither body was preserved, and no data endpoint was captured. Two of its claims survive
  only by citation inside the hearing record, and Council recorded the answers' provenance: they
  "came from **Thor, AEP, the Safety Service Director**, or any other parties directly involved."
  Treat this as the general lesson: a JavaScript-rendered municipal microsite is **not** preserved
  by the Wayback Machine.
- **The auditor parcel-number annotation** in `26-05-028.pdf` p. 2 is illegible at both 300 and
  450 DPI (bound-record-book photocopy). The four petition parcel numbers came from the hearing
  notice instead.
