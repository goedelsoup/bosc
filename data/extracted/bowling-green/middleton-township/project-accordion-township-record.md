# "Project Accordion" in the Middleton Township record, 2023–2026

The Meta campus in Wood County was assembled, zoned, abated and built through the smallest
public bodies in Ohio: a three-member board of township trustees, a county planning commission,
and a board of county commissioners. All three publish. This is what their own minutes say,
read end to end.

Issue #1438 (the record domain) · epic #1433 `readiness(bowling-green)`. Sources are committed
under [`data/documents/bowling-green/`](../../../documents/bowling-green/) with per-collection
filename maps; the structured reads are the sibling `*.resolution.yaml` files and
[`../land-assembly.yaml`](../land-assembly.yaml). Standing watches and blocked routes are in
[`../record-watch.yaml`](../record-watch.yaml).

Tags per `CLAUDE.md`: `[verified]` = printed in the body's own minutes; `[reference]` = a
statement made at a meeting or in press, journalled accurately but not adjudicated;
`[inference]` = reasoned from verified facts, shown; `[open]` = not established.

## How this was searched

All **115** minutes files Middleton Township publishes for 2023–2026 were downloaded from
`middletontownship.com/meeting_minutes/` and `.../minutes-archive-2/` and grepped. **Fifteen**
mention *Liames* or *Project Accordion*; eleven documents are committed. That matters because
several of the findings below are **negatives**, and a negative is only worth writing down when
the sweep behind it is stated.

## Four findings the chronology carries that press does not

### 1. The land was zoned in the sellers' names, before the buyer had one

A search of this township's record for "Liames" returns nothing before **2023-09-20**. But by
then the CRA application already covered "the **rezoned** properties," and by March 2024 the
zoning inspector describes Liames as owning "the **recently rezoned** parcels between St. Rt.
582, St. Rt. 25 and Mercer Road." The rezonings that made the campus possible are docketed as
**Tim Getz, ~80 acres** (2023-03-29) and the **Busch family, ~44 acres** (2023-05-03) — each
brought by the landowner, each approved before the assembly's public phase. `[verified]` on
each rezoning; `[open]` on whether either tract is inside the Liames assemblage, because the
minutes name no parcels and the auditor CAMA keeps only the last conveyance.

This is not concealment and is not written as such — a landowner rezoning his own land is
ordinary. It is a **search hazard**, and it is the reason a name-keyed sweep of a township
record will miss the first eighteen months of a project like this one.

### 2. The end use was public in September 2023, nineteen months before the reveal

The NDA concealed **who**, not **what**. On 2023-09-20 a township agenda carries "a Data Center"
and "$750M ... by a Fortune 200 company"; on 2023-09-28 the county journal carries "one or more
data center buildings on property located at State Route 582 and Mercer Road in Middleton
Township." Both name Liames LLC. Neither names Meta, and neither had to: the abatement was
granted, the schools compensated and the land rezoned on a record that described the project
accurately and its operator not at all.

### 3. Two data centers were on the record in January 2025

The register, both issue bodies and this repo's memory have all called the 2026-01-07 letter
"Meta's **Phase-2** letter." It is not — see below. The genuine multi-building signal is a year
older and comes from the township's own zoning inspector: "**The second data center is scheduled
for completion April/May 2027**," reported alongside the first data center's end-of-2026 target,
an administration building and warehouses. `[verified]` as the township's record of the
applicant's schedule.

### 4. The abatement was priced on half the jobs

Resolution 23-01249 was moved on a stated **50 full-time jobs** and a **$4,000,000** annual
payroll. Meta's own 2026-01-07 letter says "**at least 100** operational jobs." The wage implied
by the 2023 figures — $80,000 average `[inference]` — matches the mid-$80,000s now in
circulation, so what moved is the headcount, not the pay. The two figures were made by different
parties two and a half years apart, so this is **not** necessarily a revision of one estimate;
it is two numbers, and the abatement was granted against the smaller one. What the executed CRA
agreement covenants is `[open]`.

## The 2026-01-07 Meta letter, read

Three pages, signed "The Meta Global Infrastructure Team," published by the township at
`/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/`. **Its own dateline says "January 7, 2025" and is wrong by a
year** — it describes the 2025-04-09 announcement in the past tense, footnotes documents
published in August and October 2025, and was read aloud at the 2026-01-07 meeting. Content
verified to 2026-01-07; the file is not renamed.

It is a **company document**, so everything in it is Meta's account of Meta and stays
`[reference]` however specific. What the township record establishes is that it was sent and
read. On that footing it is still the most useful single source this issue found, because it is
the **primary** version of claims this repo had only from press:

- **Cooling.** "the data center will use a **closed-loop, liquid-cooled system with dry
  coolers**. This cooling system requires **no operational water**." Water is "limited to
  domestic needs, equipment cleaning, and fire protection ... sourced from the Northwestern
  Water and Sewer District." And: "**No ground water will be used**." This is the source of the
  `cooling_model=closed_loop_dry` pin, and it is now a letter to a public body rather than a
  press paraphrase. It does **not** upgrade the pin past `[reference]`: B5 (#1685) classifies
  the claim as a `reservation_conflict` against the district-linked ~600,000 gpd design
  commitment, and a self-report never resolves a conflict it is a party to.
- **A new water framing, and still no gallons.** Operational water use "is anticipated to be
  **less than what approximately two full-service restaurants use annually**." That is a third
  characterization beside the announced ~50,000 gpd and the district's ~600,000 gpd, and it
  carries no number at all. Recorded, not converted.
- **Offsite infrastructure.** Meta funded "the design and construction of a **2-million gallon
  elevated storage tank**" and "additional water distribution and **sewer conveyance** upgrade
  projects ... **along Middleton Pike**." Township counsel put a figure on the sewer side the
  same evening: **$13M**.
- **Energy, and a FirstEnergy relationship the BTM plant does not remove.** "Through our
  partnership with **First Energy**, Meta will pay for millions of dollars in new energy
  infrastructure investments — **network upgrades, utility substations, and transmission
  lines**." Read that against #1440: Apollo's certificate **bars** interconnection with the PJM
  transmission system (Condition 15), and Toledo Edison's filed tariff names Wood County but
  contains no data-center schedule. A behind-the-meter campus that is also paying for
  transmission lines is not a contradiction, but it is the largest single open question on the
  power side and this sentence sharpens it. `[reference]`.
- **The campus has its own backup generators.** "Meta runs its **backup generators** as
  minimally as possible ... and in compliance within our air permits" and is "working to fuel
  **all on site generators** with renewable diesel fuel." `SiteFacility.genset_count` and
  `genset_mw` stay `None` — the letter gives no count and no rating — but their emptiness is now
  a missing **figure**, not a possibly-absent **thing**. `[open]` on the air permit.
- **Renewables.** "more than **847 megawatts** of new renewable energy" supported in Ohio.
  `[reference]`; not reconciled against EIA-860M and not a Wood County figure.
- **Construction.** General contractor **JE Dunn**, with Ohio trade partners named: Rudolph
  Libbe, Vernon Nagel, Lake Erie Electric, Shook Construction, Valley Interiors, Xtreme
  Elements. More than one thousand construction jobs; "at least 100" operational.
- **Community.** "more than **$58 million across 2,400 grants** to date in the United States";
  a Community Action Grants program launched in Bowling Green in late 2025 with awards to be
  announced "in early Spring"; an Ohio Tech Day with OhioX and BGSU bringing "over 100 students
  from Otsego, Eastwood, and Bowling Green City schools."

**And what it does not contain: any announcement of a Phase 2.** The word does not appear. The
letter answers water, energy, air quality, construction nuisance, jobs and grants — the agenda
of the room it was read into, which had spent that evening asking about a moratorium.

## What the residents put on the record

Recorded because the completeness of a public body's comment section is itself evidence, and
because at this site the comment record is the only place some of these questions exist at all.
At the **2026-01-07** meeting: backup generators; how much energy is available; environmental
and health impact across generations; flooding and the watershed; how rezoning notice could be
widened (larger notification boundaries, signs posted at the site); noise from the power
station; whether the process is "a done deal"; landfill and wastewater experience from Lorain
County. At the **2026-07-07** hearing: buffering for Dowling Road; whether M-1 next to
residential sets a precedent for development south of SR-582; megawatt limits; the ODNR
dewatering process — to which the applicant's counsel "noted he was not familiar with the
dewatering concept."

Two of those — **dewatering** and the **City of Bowling Green's electric easement at SR-25 and
582** — are open threads this repo has not previously carried at all. They are registered as
leads in [`data/site/bowling-green/leads.yaml`](../../../site/bowling-green/leads.yaml).
