## What it is

A single bill that would create a new **R.C. Chapter 3744** — a standalone
data-center regulatory regime — and amend **R.C. 9.66** to strip the confidentiality
of data-center development-and-supply agreements. It is sponsored by Reps. Gross and
Teska (cosponsors Barhorst, Mullins, Dean, Swearingen) and declared an **emergency
measure**. It is three days old, with only "Introduced" recorded and no committee
referral. **Nothing in it is law**, and the emergency clause takes effect only *on*
passage by a two-thirds vote — so the whole memo below reads a *proposal*, not the
statute book.

## The spine: a local citizen vote

The organizing mechanism is **§3744.02** — a referendum requirement. No new or
expanded data center (its associated power plant included) may be approved until the
**electors of every city, village, or township within five miles** of the perimeter
approve it by simple majority, at an election held at least ninety days after the
question is certified. The vote must precede any permit, zoning approval, or
development-and-supply agreement; **approvals granted without it are void**; a
rejected project cannot come back for two years; and an official who bypasses the vote
commits misconduct in office (R.C. 3.07) or faces removal. A narrow carve-out (K)
spares projects already under active construction with all permits in hand before the
effective date — but a fully permitted project that has *not* broken ground must stop
until the voters approve it.

That five-mile radius is the bill's recurring unit of measure. It reappears as the
geometry for **water-supply liability** (§3744.07) and for **fine disbursement**
(§3744.08) — and it is the same buffer the platform already computes for dilution and
water-balance work.

## The environmental regime

**§3744.03** delegates to the director of environmental protection a one-year
rulemaking to set air and wastewater standards for data-center and power-plant cooling
— explicitly reaching **previously non-regulated metals, glycols, and organics
including PFAS**, plus sampling, emissions controls, and best-available-control-
technology (BACT). The director may issue **no permit** below those standards and may
grant **no waiver**.

The chapter then splits the world into **existing** and **voter-approved (new)**
facilities, hinged on the **open-loop vs closed-loop** distinction:

- **Existing centers (§3744.04, .041, .042):** eighteen months to install attached
  wastewater treatment and adopt BACT (activated carbon, ion exchange named); an
  existing **open-loop** center that falls out of compliance has its permits
  **revoked**, must run independent air-quality/public-health assessments (90 days,
  then annually) and twice-yearly cooling-tower biologic testing, and must retrofit
  emissions controls on any open-loop power plant.
- **New / voter-approved centers (§3744.05):** the director may not permit them at all
  unless they run a **closed loop** and comply with the chapter; permitting requires a
  wastewater-discharge plan, post-construction discharge testing *before* the NPDES
  permit issues, and **CEO signature authentication** of test results — a false
  pilot-scale certification is falsification under R.C. 2921.13, exposing the CEO to
  criminal prosecution.

Ongoing operation carries **chemical disclosure** to the director and the local
legislative authority with public web posting (§3744.061), independent third-party
water testing, metering of chemical additives, and weekly discharge/emissions sampling
with spring/fall Legionella control (§3744.062). The **enforcement ladder** (§3744.08)
runs from a written warning to **$10,000/day**, then **$25,000/day**, then NPDES
(Ch. 6111) and air (Ch. 3704) permit revocation — with the daily fines disbursed to
the municipalities and townships inside the five-mile radius.

## Water, grid, subsidy, and transparency

- **Water (§3744.07):** a per-day ($5,000) liability plus full remediation cost if the
  center burdens the local water supply within five miles, with a **baseline
  measurement filed to the ODNR Division of Water Resources** before construction and a
  **rebuttable presumption** of responsibility whenever measured pressure/supply later
  falls below that baseline. Aquifer impacts are named expressly.
- **Grid (§3744.09):** voter-approved and expanded centers must self-generate 100% of
  their power, operate **fully off the public grid**, and impose **no cost on Ohio
  ratepayers**.
- **Subsidy (§3744.12):** a flat ban on local tax incentives (CRA/TIF/enterprise-zone
  under R.C. Ch. 725, 1728, and the 5709 sections) and on §5715.27 tax exemptions for
  data centers.
- **Transparency (§3744.10 + §9.66(D)(2)):** all development-and-supply agreements
  become public records under **R.C. 149.43 without exception**, the §9.66(D)
  confidentiality is switched off for them, officials are protected for disclosing, and
  pre-existing agreements are **void** and disclosable. §3744.11 adds a three-year
  OPSB/PUCO revolving-door bar.

## Why it matters to the corpus

This is the rare instrument that touches almost every domain the network models, and
it does so by converting the platform's empirical observations into statutory triggers:

- The **five-mile radius** for water burden and the **ODNR baseline / rebuttable
  presumption** are the exact geometry and mechanism of the well-drawdown / dewatering
  work and the routed water balance — the "goes-dry vulnerability" and the discharge
  screen map straight onto the presumption's baseline comparison.
- The **open-loop / closed-loop** gate is the statutory form of the closed-loop-cooling-
  cycling question: the bill would make a new center's permit contingent on a closed
  loop and revoke an open-loop existing center's permits on non-compliance.
- The **NPDES / air revocation ladder** and the **PFAS/metals/glycol** cooling
  chemistry bear on the thermal-discharge epic, the NPDES ingest, and the AERMET/AERMAP
  air pipeline; the previously-unregulated-chemical limits are a new surface for the
  water-quality-standards screen.
- The **grid-independence** and **no-ratepayer-cost** mandate is the legislative
  counterpart to the grid-power domain and the ratepayer-cost dispute already in the
  `select-committee-2026` record; the **revolving-door** bar bears on the OPSB
  siting-case work.
- The **citizen vote** generalizes the site-level moratoria and zoning fights the
  network tracks (Mansfield's rejection; Defiance's and Springfield's moratoria); the
  **tax-incentive ban** and the **transparency pair** target the CRA/TIF and DCTE
  subsidy levers the select-committee record disputes.
- The **§9.66(D)(2) / §3744.10** transparency pair is the same confidentiality lever the
  H.B. 314 records-legislation digest and the mandamus analysis flag — here pulled in
  the opposite direction, toward disclosure.
