# West Union / Adams County, OH — Data-Center Activity Register

Discover-and-pin register for the West Union watershed point (basin `ohio-brush-creek`; Ohio
River direct branch). Sweep status **as of 2026-08-05** (#1983). Tags are BOSC evidentiary
discipline: `[verified]` = a cited primary instrument or an authoritative agency dataset,
`[reference]` = credible media / secondary source, `[inference]`, `[open]`.

**This sweep had never been run.** #1983 was written on the premise that Adams County carried
"no disclosed project" and that the premise had never been tested. It has now been tested, and
**the premise is wrong**: there is a disclosed data-center campus in Adams County, and Adams
County's own government domain names its operator as **Amazon Web Services** `[verified]`.

Separately, AES Ohio has filed a PJM load request **near its Stuart Substation in Adams County
for an unnamed customer**, ramping to **1,300 MW by 03/2032** `[verified]` — which, if built,
would be the largest single data-center load on the Ohio public record. **The filing names no
customer, and no instrument joins it to the AWS campus.** That the two are the same project is a
strong but unfiled **`[inference]`** — see "The evidentiary spine" below, which is the finding of
this register. Nothing here should be read as Amazon having disclosed a megawatt figure; it has
not.

Nothing in this register is in the BOSC corpus yet — no source document under
`data/documents/` is cited below. This records the *verified public record* and names the
instruments to pull. Do not bridge the Lima/Allen County graph, the Van Wert/QTS graph or the
Urbana/Thor graph onto Adams County; there is no evidentiary link to any of them.

  **1022 Ginger Ridge Rd, Manchester, OH 45144** — *not* in or near the Village of West Union

  River, under **separate ownership** and separate redevelopment tracks. They are routinely
  conflated in coverage. `[verified]`
- ⚠️ **Standing conflation guard (carried from #1353):** the "100 MW → 1.3 GW AES Ohio ramp" is

### Water / hydrology hook

- **Cooling water source: the Ohio River.** The county/AWS page answers the question directly:
  *"The water that will serve the data center's cooling needs will come from the Ohio River."*
  `[verified]`
- **Projected cooling water use: ~19.7 million gallons per year** (~54,000 GPD average).
  `[verified]`
- **Domestic water: a maximum of 40,000 gallons per day**, from the **Adams County Regional
  Water District (ACRWD)**, stated to come from ACRWD's existing surplus capacity. `[verified]`
- **Cooling design: air-cooled ~97% of the year**, water-based **evaporative** cooling **<3% of
  annual operations**, during peak summer temperatures. AWS states this reduces electricity
  demand 25–35% at summer peak. `[verified]` — this is the **hybrid/adiabatic** archetype, not
  closed-loop-dry and not open evaporative.
- **Wastewater:** the brochure states discharges are "treated by the city's wastewater facility."
  **Which POTW is `[open]`** — the campus is in Sprigg Township, and the Village of Manchester
  and Village of West Union are separate systems; the West Union WWTP (`0PC00019` /
  `OH0028088`, design 0.7 MGD, discharging to Beasley Fork) is ~15 mi away and is almost
  certainly *not* the receiving POTW. Do not assume it. `[open]`
- **Intake instrument: `[open]`.** An Ohio River withdrawal of this kind needs a constructed
  intake; no USACE Section 10 authorization, ODNR water-withdrawal registration, or Ohio River
  Valley Water Sanitation Commission record has been located. Site marketing separately claims
  **two on-site wells at 720,000 GPD each** and "permits to be obtained" for river withdrawal.
  `[reference]`

### Hydrology screen — ⚠️ the committed screen is route-blind to this facility

- The site profile screens on **`ohio-brush-creek`**, with `abstraction_gage` and
  `supply_gage_primary` both **USGS `03237500` (Ohio Brush Creek near West Union, DA 387 mi²)**.
- **The campus does not drain to Ohio Brush Creek.** Its jurisdictional streams are **Elk Run
  and Carter Hollow** (USACE record, `[verified]`) and its cooling withdrawal is from the **Ohio
  River mainstem** (`[verified]`). Sprigg Township sits in the county's southwest corner and
  drains directly to the Ohio River, outside the Ohio Brush Creek drainage. `[inference]` —
  confirm against NHDPlus flowlines / HUC-12 before relying on it.
- **Consequence:** any dilution or withdrawal screen run against `03237500` would be answering
  the wrong question for this facility. **A 19.7 Mgal/yr consumptive draw on the Ohio River
  mainstem is hydrologically trivial** (the county page puts it at <0.0001% of annual flow) —
  the binding constraint at this campus is **power and land use, not water abstraction**, the
  same methodological correction #1330 forced at Urbana.
- **Nothing on the profile should be re-pointed on this finding alone.** The watershed point is
  the Village of West Union on Ohio Brush Creek, and that is still what the site *is*. But the
  site's headline facility is not in its screened basin, and that must be stated wherever the
  two are shown together. `[verified]`
- **7Q10 for the Ohio River at/near Manchester: `[open]`** — not in
  `data/reference/hydrology/low-flow-7q10*.yaml`, cited or derived.

## 3 — Governance posture: the county is essentially unzoned

This is the sharpest contrast in the network. At Findlay, **Allen Township §1521 caps data
centers at 10 MW** over a 150 MW site. **Adams County has no such instrument to apply.**

- Petitions circulated **2026-03-09** ask the **county commissioners to establish "rural
  zoning"** — i.e. **no county zoning currently exists**. `[reference]`
- **Sprigg Township** (the Stuart plant / Buck Canyon township) passed a **one-year "voluntary
  moratorium" on data centers on 2026-03-02**, after a **2026-01-26 resolution signalling "no
  intent" to change zoning.** A *voluntary* moratorium in an unzoned township is a **statement of
  posture with no land-use force** — it does not gate a permit. `[reference]` / `[inference]`
- **Monroe Township** (the Killen plant) passed a **2026-03-02 resolution establishing a zoning
  commission** over its 27-sq-mi jurisdiction. `[reference]`
- **Consequence, and the reason the federal and state permits are the whole record:** with no
  county zoning and no township zoning in force at the campus, **there is no local land-use
  approval in this project's path.** That is why this register's instruments are USACE, Ohio EPA
  and PJM — and why there are no council resolutions, rezonings or annexations to pull, unlike
  Van Wert (#1401) or Wilmington (#1470). `[inference]`

### The statewide ballot track — filed, and failed, out of this county

- **"Prohibition of Construction of a Data Center"** — an initiated Ohio constitutional
  amendment, led by **Brown and Adams County residents** (Conserve Ohio). It would **prohibit
  construction of data centers with aggregate power demand exceeding 25 MW.** `[verified]`
- The **Ohio Ballot Board voted unanimously** to keep it as a single amendment; petitioners were
  cleared to circulate ~2026-04-03. `[reference]`
- Required **413,488 valid signatures by 2026-07-01**, from half of Ohio's 88 counties.
- **It did not qualify.** ~**70,000** signatures as of 2026-06-18. The signatures were **not
  submitted** to the Secretary of State and therefore **remain valid**; the campaign is
  targeting **2027**. `[reference]`
- **Standing watch:** a 25 MW threshold would reach this campus by a factor of ~52. A 2027
  re-filing is a live trigger.

## 5 — Method note: how this sweep was run

The #1983 acceptance criteria assume the agentic path (`watermark onboard west-union
--research` → `data/research/<slug>-<date>/`). **That path was not used**, and no
`data/research/onboard-west-union-*` directory is committed by this work — writing one would
mean fabricating the agent-run metadata (`model`, `turns`, `cost_usd`, `tools_used`) that the
existing manifests carry truthfully.

The sweep was run **manually against primary sources on 2026-08-05**, following the
`data-center-sweep` skill's source-priority order. Routes exercised, in order:

1. County / municipal record — `adamscountyoh.gov` (the AWS subdomain **is** the county's
   publication) and township resolutions via local press.
2. County recorder / auditor — **not reached**; deed and parcel IDs remain `[open]`.
3. Ohio Secretary of State — **blocked** (not a vetted connector host, #1950).
4. Ohio EPA — the **DSW Construction and Industrial NOI general-permit lists** (authoritative
   agency datasets, report date 2026-08-05), pulled and read directly. This is what produced
   `0GC04922*AG` and the transmission table.
5. Federal — USACE NWP 39 verification (via the local record).
6. Utility / RTO — **PJM TEAC Dayton Supplemental deck of 2026-02-03**, pulled and read
   directly. This is the primary instrument for the load.
7. Trade and local press — WCPO I-Team, The People's Defender, Ohio Capital Journal, Statehouse
   News Bureau (`[reference]` throughout).

> documents**, now shelved at [`data/documents/oepa/west-union/`](../../documents/oepa/west-union/).

> `2GC08872` chain; the negative control returned 0. What the 2026-08-05 attempt got wrong is not

> (`ddlValue_-1_1_104_1` / `ddlValue_-1_1_109_1`), not the postback-only pickers the earlier note
> assumed. The pull delivered exactly what this section said it would: the `0GC04922*AG` NOI, its

> ⚠️ The lesson is the one this section already states in its own title, pointed the other way: a

The attempt was controlled, and the controls are the finding:

| Query | Result |

  rare on this network; Amazon states the mechanism (97% air / <3% evaporative) directly.

**`it_load_mw` is deliberately left `None` — the load is entirely `[open]`.** This is the model's
explicit affordance (`SiteProfile.has_facility_power_basis`), not an oversight. The 1,300 MW is
AES Ohio's filing for an **unnamed** customer; `it_load_mw` feeds arithmetic (`facility_draw = IT
× PUE`, then the demand-pressure share of state retail), so keying it in would publish, as
structured data, a load Amazon has never disclosed and no instrument attributes to it — while
this register's central finding is precisely that no instrument joins the two. Consequently
`derive_power_basis` returns `None`, the grid stack emits the AEP-zone backdrop with
`load_share=None`, and the demand-pressure command skips cleanly rather than sizing a fabricated
campus. Fill it in as `PERMIT`/`DISCLOSURE` when an instrument names **both** operator and load.

Downstream, `readiness.inquiry` also rose **`seeded` → `live`**: with a facility on the record the
study's `project` and `balance` chapters became substantive (9 of 15, against a threshold of 8,
with the record-keyed `assembly` chapter substantive). The chapters that flipped `na` → `gap`
(air, groundwater, stormwater, water-supply, heat, fiscal) are the honest move — those questions
are now *applicable and unanswered* rather than *not applicable*. **The tier is unchanged at
`backdrop`**, since it derives from the four record-bearing domains and `places` is still
`absent` (no committed geometry — see the blocked site-map pull above).
