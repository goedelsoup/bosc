# Maumee-at-Waterville (USGS 04193500) — continuous-monitor read vs the Napoleon spill

Sub-issue #1498 of the Napoleon / Huston Creek water-watch (#1497). The Maumee-at-Waterville
gage — Toledo's monitor and the basin's derived 7Q10 reference — sits ~42 river-km downstream of
the Huston Creek mouth and ran through the whole event. This is the read of its continuous record
against the spill timeline, and the verdict on what is **attributable** to the release versus
merely **coincident** with the storm.

The machine-readable read (extrema, spike timestamps, travel time, 7Q10 dilution) is
`data/reference/hydrology/toledo/waterville-spill-monitor-read.yaml`, regenerated by
`watermark waterville-monitor --write`. The raw sub-daily record is frozen as the committed NWIS IV
fixture `tests/fixtures/hydrology/nwis/b7df4628e1a85dff.json` (NWIS IV service, site `04193500`,
parameters `00060,00095,00300,00010,63680,32316,32319`, `2026-07-01..2026-07-12`) because the
instantaneous-value service retains only ~120 days.

## The event clock

- **[verified]** ~Jul 3 — contamination reported in Huston Creek (Triway Nitrogen fertilizer via
  Farmers Elevator Associates). Containment dams installed ~Jul 6. (#1497)
- **[verified]** Overnight **Fri Jul 10 → Sat Jul 11** — a containment dam **failed under heavy
  rainfall**, releasing the impounded fertilizer to the Maumee. (#1497)
- **[verified]** Weekend Jul 11–12 — dead fish from the Huston Creek mouth to the Campbell's Soup
  railroad bridge. (#1497)

## The reads

**The loud signal pre-dates the release — it is storm first-flush, not the plume. [inference]**
The two turbidity spikes are Jul 10 midday/evening. The dam failed *overnight Jul 10 → Jul 11*.
A plume released after the spikes cannot, at any positive travel time, produce them. The
Huston-mouth → Waterville travel time is **~21–32 h** (41.9 river-km by NLDI upstream-main
navigation 04193500→04192550; mean advective velocity ~1.2–1.8 ft/s from Manning normal-depth over
a plausible lower-Maumee geometry at the storm flows), so the dam-failure plume's leading edge only
reaches Waterville **late Jul 11 – Jul 12**, at the very edge of the record. The spikes are ordinary
first-flush turbidity / resuspension from the same rain that failed the dam.

**No dissolved-plume signature is captured in-window. [inference]** For a dissolved
ammonium-nitrate release, specific conductance is a closer surrogate than turbidity — yet
conductance *fell* (storm dilution) on Jul 10 and returned to its 535–592 µS/cm normal through
Jul 11–12, with no anomalous rise. Because the expected plume arrival lands at the window edge, this
is "not captured," not "did not occur."

**The initial spill week had a tight assimilative denominator. [inference]** Waterville flow
bottomed at 424 cfs (Jul 5–6), only **3.7×** the derived Maumee 7Q10 (114.15 cfs). Any Huston Creek
input during that week met minimal dilution — the low-flow condition the assimilative screen keys
on. The rain that failed the dam also raised dilution ~10× by Jul 8.

**The DO sag is thermal, not the spill. [inference]** The sag to 4.3 mg/L (Jul 5) coincides with
the 35.5 °C heat and the low-flow trough, and it **pre-dates** the dam failure by ~5 days. Noted,
not attributed.

## Discipline

- **[open]** Turbidity, fPC and specific conductance are **optical/ionic surrogates**. 04193500
  carries **no ammonia or nitrate probe**, so the reach's nitrogen signal — the thing the fish kill
  actually turns on — must come from the OEPA / Napoleon grab samples (the record sub-issue), never
  from this monitor.
- **Correction to the brief:** #1498 cited pcode **32316** as fPC. 32316 is **chlorophyll-a**;
  phycocyanin (fPC) is **32319 / 32321**. The fPC value the brief quoted (3.36 µg/L @ Jul 10 12:00)
  is real on 32319 but is a mid-range diel reading, below the month's Jul 1–2 peaks — not an
  isolated spill spike.
- The travel-time bracket rests on the **spike-vs-release ordering**, which is robust to the
  channel-geometry assumptions; the width of the ~21–32 h range is not load-bearing for the verdict.
